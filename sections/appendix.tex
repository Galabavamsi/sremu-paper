\section{Oracle Details}\label{app:oracles}
\claim{C-ORACLES}The tests of \sremu{} form four tiers. Analytic oracles (identifiers PH,
RT and IO) compare the code with closed forms or exact properties in double or exact
arithmetic and run in continuous integration. Statistical oracles (ST) run seeded Monte Carlo
experiments with stated confidence levels. Code-to-code references (G1--G8) compare the
emulator with stored outputs of MATLAB R2026a scripts (those of the RIS kernel were written
for K1); MATLAB is not needed to run them, and their agreement shows agreement between two
codes, not validity.
Validation and reproduction studies (V) use published or measured data.

\claim{C-ORACLE-INDEPENDENCE}The closed forms live in a separate test package that was
written from independent derivations and scripts, not from the emulator's code, and that may
not import the physics, channel or impairment packages; a static check of the imports and a
check in a fresh interpreter enforce this. The physics oracles cover the Friis equation, image
theory for plates and disks, the monostatic radar cross section of a plate, the
far-field gain of a focused panel, focusing saturation, Fresnel reflection and the two-ray
model, the knife-edge Fresnel integral, the losses of finite alphabets and of the
phase-dependent amplitude model, and exhaustive searches for the exact optimum. The runtime
oracles cover exact sample counts and round trips at several incommensurate rates, the switch
sample of a distant receiver, bitwise invariance under chunking, cuts, skipped pages and
thread counts, closed-form transition profiles and the paced-mode counters; property tests
draw random rational rates, times and periods. The interface oracles feed malformed frames
and messages, check the size and element limits and the loopback rule, and check the
structure of the SigMF recordings. The statistical oracles test the stationary distribution
of the random-waypoint model, the autocorrelation and level-crossing rate of the diffuse term
against Clarke's model~\cite{Clarke1968}, and the Monte Carlo losses of quantization and
failures.

\claim{C-TEETH}Several guards have a negative-control twin: a test that runs a deliberately
wrong variant and must reject it, for example a kernel without the factor $j$, a
fractional-delay filter that is too short, or a replay that drops a command. A guard that accepts its wrong
variant is broken. Section~\ref{sec:vv} and Appendix~\ref{app:vv} summarize every check with
its status as recorded in the test record of commit \txtProvTestCommit{}
(Appendix~\ref{app:repro}); a spec identifier counts as
passed when at least one of its implementation tests passed and none failed or was skipped,
and the tests of the rendering path count once per backend.

\claim{C-ID-TABLE}Tables~\ref{tab:ids-ph} and~\ref{tab:ids-rt} list every verification
identifier cited in this paper with the property it checks, its oracle or reference and its
tolerance. Apart from VA-1 and VA-4 on the batch path, Sections~\ref{sec:design}
and~\ref{sec:model} cite no identifiers in place, so the tables also name, for each identifier, the subsections of
those sections whose statements it checks, taken from the evidence that the pipeline's claim
file records for each statement and from the identifiers cited there. They are generated from a transcription that the pipeline checks against the
emulator's specification, its table of oracle values and the docstrings of the tests that
carry each identifier: a number found in none of them, an identifier cited without a row and
an identifier that did not pass in the test record all stop the pipeline. The VA identifiers
(VA-1, VA-3, VA-4 and VA-5, the complete list of the specification) are consistency checks between
the engine's paths and run in the test record beside the four tiers.

\claim{C-REPRO}The paper is built from two repositories, the emulator and the paper, by a
Python pipeline rather than by hand. The paper's lock file names the emulator commit that
holds the results. In the
emulator repository, \texttt{python tools/tasks.py paper-junit FILE} runs the test tiers and
writes a test record with the commit and the state of the working tree. In the paper
repository, the single command
\texttt{python tools/build\_paper.py -{}-mode final -{}-profile full -{}-junit FILE} then rebuilds
every stale figure and table and compiles the paper. It refuses to produce a PDF unless the
installed emulator is the locked commit, both working trees are clean, every identifier of
both verification gates passed in a test record of the locked code, every figure is current,
every number is a macro written by the pipeline and every claim is supported. A figure is
current when its stored content key, a hash of the emulator commit, the sweep specification,
the input files and the figure script, matches. The constants and defaults quoted in
Sections~\ref{sec:design} and~\ref{sec:model} and Appendices~\ref{app:interfaces}
and~\ref{app:model} are read from the locked code at build time,
so the text cannot drift from the code. Every run directory carries the manifest of
Section~\ref{sec:design-arch}, and a replay reproduces its received samples bit for bit. The
build checks the lock commit, the test record and the figure content keys; the results
themselves record the commit, tree state and host they were produced on, and
Table~\ref{tab:provenance} lists them. Neither repository is public yet; the section Code and
Data Availability states the conditions of their release.

\claim{C-REPRO-RUNS}The results produced at emulator commit \txtProvRegenCommit{} from a clean
tree are \txtProvRegenResults; all of them except the benchmarks come from one regeneration job
on an \txtProvRegenHost{} instance, and the benchmarks are measurements on dedicated hosts, run
as separate jobs (Appendix~\ref{app:bench}). The test record was made at
\txtProvTestCommit{}, and the build checks that the installed emulator's package source (a hash
over all its modules) equals the one these results record. Each result keeps the commit at which
it was produced (Table~\ref{tab:provenance}); for the other results these are
\txtProvOtherCommits. V-LAB is not rerun, because its analysis plan is locked: it ran once, at
\txtLabCodeCommit{} from a clean tree, on predictions computed before the lock. A check at
\txtProvTestCommit{} found the V-LAB protocol and analysis files byte-identical to those of
\txtLabCodeCommit{} and the protocol hash equal to the lock; emulator modules that the analysis
imports have changed since, so we do not claim that a rerun would reproduce it.

\section{Data Provenance}\label{app:provenance}
\claim{C-PROVENANCE}Every input and every figure carries data classes: MEAS (measured
data), DIG (digitized from a figure, with its digitization uncertainty), REPORTED
(quoted from a publication), ASSUMED (chosen where no data exist, such as the amplitudes of
the laboratory states), ENG (an engineering choice made for a study, such as receiver
positions or the transition shape), SIM-sremu (outputs of this emulator), ANALYTIC (closed
forms) and MATLAB (code-to-code references). Run manifests list the hash and data class of
every input, and every figure caption names the classes it shows. Input files are copied into
the repositories with their hashes and provenance; data not yet cleared for publication are
read from a private location, and the tests that need them are skipped when it is absent.
Response tables keep their own provenance (Section~\ref{sec:design-states}). The laboratory
measurement of Section~\ref{sec:vv-lab} is a measurement collected by the authors and enters
as a digitized map (DIG); Appendix~\ref{app:vv} states its origin. Process records carry no
physical quantity: GitHub's push log of
the emulator repository, quoted in Section~\ref{sec:vv-lab}, is kept as an exported copy with
its hash. Table~\ref{tab:provenance} lists the producing commit, tree state, host and date of
every result. The digitized measurements of Tang et al.\ enter as DIG with the
reported link budget (REPORTED); Appendix~\ref{app:tang} breaks their comparison with the
emulator (Section~\ref{sec:vv-tang}) down by geometry.
