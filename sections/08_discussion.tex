\subsection{Alignment with standards}\label{sec:disc-standards}
\input{sections/tables/table6_standards}
\claim{C-STANDARDS}Table~\ref{tab:standards} maps the terms and models of the ETSI documents
on RIS to elements of \sremu{}. ETSI GR RIS 002~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS002_v121} separates the RIS
controller, which generates control information, from the micro-controller, which configures
the elements. \sremu{} keeps this split: controllers decide, and the engine applies their
commands with the panel's control granularity, quantizer and failure map at the sample of
each receiver. The general-case path-loss model of ETSI GR RIS 003~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS003_v121}
equals K1 for one point per cell, and its reradiation integral has the factor $j$ that K1
keeps; its mutually coupled antenna model and its polarized elements are outside the model
of \sremu{}. The parameters of ETSI GS RIS 008~\cite{ETSI_GS_RIS008} appear as fields of the
response tables and of the panel capabilities. GS RIS 008 measures the reconfiguration time
from the receipt of a command to the effective configuration; in \sremu{} that interval is
the command latency, which is drawn or measured and logged per command, plus the transition,
whose default duration is the panel's reconfiguration time. The modulated-signal quality of
GS RIS 008, the error vector magnitude (EVM), is measured by an RX application on received
samples; Section~\ref{sec:cases} uses it. \claim{C-TSDSI}The text of TSDSI STD
5003~\cite{TSDSI_STD5003} was not accessed; no alignment is claimed. The E2 service model for
RIS used in O-RAN
frameworks~\cite{ElGhalbzouri2025GoSimRIS,Kayraklik2026ORIX} is not implemented.

\subsection{Hooks for learning-based control}\label{sec:disc-ml}
\claim{C-CONTROLLER-API}Learning-based control is outside the scope of this paper, but
\sremu{} provides three hooks for it: a controller protocol, an environment with a standard
learning interface, and a regret reference. A controller
has a name, a period, a minimum latency, an observation specification and a latency model,
and two methods: \texttt{setup} receives the panel capabilities and a keyed random generator,
and \texttt{decide} maps an observation to actions. The same object runs inside the engine at
barriers or in an external process (\texttt{run\_remote}), and both give identical
received samples (RT-08), so a controller tested in process can be deployed as a separate
process without changes. The built-in
controllers are a fixed configuration; RIS absent; the uniform random activation and our
reading of the position-driven heuristic of~\cite{Mishra2025WCL}; the exact optimum; phase
alignment followed by quantization; a design for an ideal alphabet deployed on the true
states; a cyclic codebook sweep; an upper-confidence-bound (UCB1) bandit~\cite{Auer2002UCB}
over windowed received power; a genetic algorithm; a controller that alternates
configurations between the packets of two receivers; and a wrapper that delays the position
reports of any position-driven controller. Every random draw of a controller comes from its
keyed generator, so its decisions are reproducible.

\claim{C-RISENV}The environment, \texttt{RisEnv}, wraps a scenario in the reset/step
interface of Gymnasium~\cite{Towers2024Gymnasium}; it subclasses Gymnasium's
environment class when that package is installed and imitates it otherwise. An action is a
state vector or a codebook index, applied after a configurable latency; the reward is the
received pilot-tone power over the decision period; the samplers of the action and
observation spaces and the scene are keyed by the reset seed. In this release the
environment computes rewards on the batch path; its IQ backends are not yet connected to the
engine. \claim{C-REGRET}For the regret reference, the environment reports at every step the exact finite-alphabet optimum (Section~\ref{sec:model-nonideal}), computed
from oracle channel state at the same evaluation times, and the gap between that optimum and
the achieved power. The gap is zero for an optimal controller; it ignores panel failures, so
it can be negative when cells are dead. The optimum bounds every built-in controller at every
tested position (VA-3).

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:disc-limits}
\input{sections/tables/table_limitations}
\claim{C-LIMITS}Table~\ref{tab:limitations} lists what the current release does not model or
provide.
Three limits matter most for reading the results of this paper. The paced mode is soft real
time: it counts deadline misses and does not prevent them. Agreement with MATLAB is a
comparison of two codes and does not validate absolute RIS power; validation needs
measurements (Section~\ref{sec:vv}). And mutual coupling between elements enters solely
through measured or simulated tables of whole configurations, so a controller cannot exploit
coupling that its tables do not contain.
