\section{Physical Model}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Scene, snapshots and conventions}\label{sec:model-stack}
% physics/geometry.py (Static, Waypoints, ConstantVelocity, RandomWaypoint1D/2D,
% TraceTrajectory, ExternalTrajectory), physics/propagation.py (snapshot, combine; B[r,s,n]
% or separable C[r,n] with gamma[s]; rows = (link, hop variant)), contracts.py conventions.
\claim{C-STACK}Node positions follow analytic trajectories in continuous time: static,
piecewise-linear waypoints, constant velocity, one- and two-dimensional random
waypoint~\cite{Bettstetter2003RWP} with keyed legs, a cubic-spline trace, or timed updates
from a client. Positions therefore never depend on an update grid. At a snapshot time $t$ the
propagation model evaluates, in double precision, the paths of every link that do not
involve a RIS and, for every RIS panel, a coefficient tensor $B[r,s,n]$ over rows $r$ (a link
together with a ground variant of its two hops), states $s$ and physical cells $n$; tables
without angle dependence use a separable form. A configuration selects one state per cell,
and $\mathrm{combine}$ then forms one path per panel, row and render tile; this step is
linear in $\Gamma$. Channel coefficients are dimensionless and normalized as in the Friis
equation, $P_R=P_T|h|^2$, with antenna gains inside $h$. The complex baseband signal $x(t)$
of a carrier $f_c$ is $s(t)=\sqrt2\,\mathrm{Re}\{x(t)e^{j2\pi f_ct}\}$, and a path with delay
$\tau$ maps $x(t)$ to $a\,x(t-\tau)\,e^{-j2\pi f_c\tau}$.

\claim{C-LOS}The line-of-sight (LOS) path is $h_{\mathrm{LOS}}=\sqrt{G_TG_R}\,
\lambda/(4\pi d)\,V$ with scalar co-polar antenna patterns (isotropic, a $\cos^\alpha$
pattern with peak gain $2(\alpha+1)$, or a tabulated pattern with provenance) and an explicit
visibility factor $V$: zero behind an opaque occluder, $10^{-L/20}$ through a partition with
transmission loss $L$~dB (an engineering choice), or the Fresnel knife-edge factor $F(\nu)$
of one edge, which keeps its phase and adds coherently to the RIS paths. Scenario names
never imply blockage. Optional mechanisms add a flat-ground image path with Fresnel
coefficients, image expansion of the two RIS hops over that ground, order-one images in
finite walls and an additive diffuse term (Table~\ref{tab:modelcard}).

\subsection{RIS kernel K1}\label{sec:model-k1}
% physics/ris_kernel.py (k1_terms, accumulate_cells; obliquity sqrt default, kirchhoff
% option with ValidityWarning, none for includes_cell_pattern; huygens j always on;
% visibility indicator); physics/propagation.py (B tensor phase relative to tile reference).
\claim{C-K1}The RIS contribution of every link is the area-tied physical-optics kernel K1
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
h_{\mathrm{RIS}}={}&\sum_{q}\sqrt{G_T(\hat r^{i}_q)\,G_R(-\hat r^{s}_q)}\;
  \Gamma_{n(q)}(s,f,\theta_{iq},\theta_{sq})\\
&\times\frac{A_q\,o(\theta_{iq},\theta_{sq})}{4\pi\, r_{iq}\, r_{sq}}\;
  j\,e^{-jk(r_{iq}+r_{sq})}\\
&\times\mathbb{1}[\cos\theta_{iq}>0,\ \cos\theta_{sq}>0],
\end{split}
\label{eq:k1}
\end{equation}
where $q$ runs over quadrature points (one per physical cell, or sub-cells, see
Section~\ref{sec:model-cells}) and $n(q)$ is the cell that carries the state and $\Gamma$;
$A_q$ is the pitch area of the quadrature cell, gaps between cells included, so the areas of
the sub-cells of one cell add up to its pitch area; $r_{iq}$ and $r_{sq}$ are the distances
from transmitter and receiver to the point; $\theta_{iq}$ and $\theta_{sq}$ are the angles
toward them from the panel normal; $\hat r^i_q$ and $\hat r^s_q$ are the unit vectors from
transmitter to point and from point to receiver; $o=\sqrt{\cos\theta_i\cos\theta_s}$ is the
default obliquity, the cosine pattern measured by Tang et al.~\cite{Tang2022mmWave}; $j$ is the
Huygens (physical-optics) factor; and the indicator keeps front-side incidence and reflection,
so a path to the back of a panel contributes exactly zero (PH-09).

\claim{C-K1-AREA}Two properties of K1 matter for every result in this paper. The
contribution of each cell is tied to its area, so subdividing a fixed aperture into more cells
leaves the received power unchanged (PH-04, Section~\ref{sec:vv-tang}); a kernel with a fixed
per-cell gain would instead grow with every subdivision. The factor $j$ sets the phase of the
RIS path relative to the LOS path; without it the phase would be wrong by a quarter turn,
which a comparison with image theory detects (PH-05). \claim{C-K1-FORMS}For one quadrature point per cell, K1 is algebraically
equal to three published forms: the cell model of Tang et al.~\cite{Tang2022mmWave} times
$j$, the general path-loss model of ETSI GR RIS 003~\cite[clause 6.3.1.2]{ETSI_GR_RIS003_v121}
with its recommended cell gain $G=4\pi d_xd_y/\lambda^2$, and the model of
Ellingson~\cite{Ellingson2021PathLoss} with an element gain tied to the cell area,
$G_e(\psi)=(4\pi A/\lambda^2)\cos\psi$. A test checks the equality to double precision
(PH-02). \claim{C-K1-GUARDS}A scenario check warns when a cell is closer to a node than
three wavelengths, where the point-scatterer approximation weakens, and rejects distances
below one wavelength. With the default obliquity the scattered power of a passive panel does
not exceed the power it intercepts in any tested configuration (PH-23); the alternative obliquity
$(\cos\theta_i+\cos\theta_s)/2$ can exceed it for anomalous reflection and therefore
emits a validity warning when selected.

\subsection{Cell model}\label{sec:model-cells}
% physics/quadrature.py (resolve_cell_model, build_quadrature, representative_links,
% richardson_check); scenario/models.py Numerics defaults.
\claim{C-CELL-MODEL}Each panel selects one of three cell models. \texttt{point} places one
quadrature point per physical cell; it is the model that Tang et al.\ compared with their
measurements~\cite{Tang2022mmWave}. \texttt{po\_patch} splits each cell into sub-cells with
sides of at most \numSubcellMax$\lambda$ that share the cell's $\Gamma$, a uniform-patch
physical-optics model. \texttt{auto}, the default, selects \texttt{point} when the cell pitch
is at most \numPointMaxPitch$\lambda$ and \texttt{po\_patch} otherwise. Forcing
\texttt{point} on larger cells emits a warning, and tables whose measurement already contains
the cell pattern force \texttt{point} without obliquity, so that the pattern is not counted
twice. The two models are not numerically equivalent: a patch carries the sub-aperture factor
of a uniform patch, which a point omits. They agree near specular reflection and differ by
several decibels for half-wavelength cells at wide non-specular angles (PH-26); we treat
this difference as model-form uncertainty. \claim{C-RICHARDSON}For \texttt{po\_patch}
panels a Richardson check computes the received power of up to \numRichardsonLinks{}
representative links at sub-cell size $\Delta$ and at $\Delta/2$, halves $\Delta$ at most
\numRichardsonHalvings{} times while the change exceeds \numRichardsonTolDb~dB, and fails
otherwise; the accepted size and the estimate go to the manifest.

\subsection{Element states and non-idealities}\label{sec:model-nonideal}
% physics/nonideal.py (pipeline order, quantize_phases, FailureMap, efficiency_factor,
% CouplingHook without kappa values, binary_md); physics/optimize.py (exact optimum).
\claim{C-NONIDEAL}The reflection coefficient of each cell comes from its response table
(Section~\ref{sec:design-states}) through a fixed pipeline. A command (state indices, phases
or a codebook entry) is expanded from controls to physical cells by the panel's control
granularity. Continuous phases are quantized by
$s=\arg\max_s\mathrm{Re}\{\bar\Gamma(s)e^{-j\phi}\}$, which stays correct when the states
have unequal amplitudes (PH-30). Stuck and dead cells follow a failure map; dead cells absorb
by default. The table is evaluated at each cell's angles. An efficiency factor scales the
amplitude by $\sqrt{\eta}$; it is refused for measured or digitized tables unless explicitly
overridden, because their data already contain the loss. A coupling hook can add a
state-dependent correction, but no coupling coefficients are shipped: coupling enters
through measured or simulated per-configuration tables~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS003_v121}. The
transition of Section~\ref{sec:design-time} acts in time. Closed-form losses of finite
alphabets and the mirror and steerable parts of a binary alphabet, $\Gamma=m+ds$ with
$s=\pm1$, check this pipeline (PH-13, PH-15, PH-25).

\claim{C-OPTIMUM}An exact maximizer of $|z_0+\sum_n Z[s_n,n]|$ over any finite alphabet
serves as an upper bound for controllers and studies. For a direction $\theta$ the best
per-element choice is $s_n(\theta)=\arg\max_s\mathrm{Re}\{Z[s,n]e^{-j\theta}\}$; it can
change at the critical angles $\arg(Z[a,n]-Z[b,n])\pm\pi/2$ and nowhere else, and the
optimum is the choice at the direction of the optimal sum. A sweep over the sorted critical
angles therefore finds the optimum in $O(S^2N\log(S^2N))$ operations for $S$ states and $N$
cells, and it matches exhaustive search in every tested instance (PH-24, PH-29). The method
is not new: optimal discrete-phase beamforming has been solved
before~\cite{Zhang2022OptimalBinary,Zhao2024OptimalDiscrete}.

\subsection{Mobility, Doppler and interpolation}\label{sec:model-doppler}
% channel/interp.py (EPS_REL split D1, t_curv_s, tile_side_max_m, choose_policy, M_MAX,
% T_U_MAX_S, VERIFY_INTERP_STRIDE, VERIFY_INTERP_FLAG, verify_interp); engine/scheduler.py
% (snapshot grid, switches never interpolated across).
\claim{C-DOPPLER}Doppler shifts are not parameters: they follow from the geometry. Snapshots
are evaluated at $t_k=kT_u$ on an absolute grid (for static scenes at events). Between two
snapshots each path without a RIS has a chord delay, linear in time between its delays at
the two snapshots, and the carrier phase that is exact for that delay,
$e^{-j2\pi f_c\tau_p(t)}$; its amplitude is complex-linear. Each RIS panel row is split into
render tiles. A tile has a reference delay, rendered like a path, and a residual: the sum of
its cells' coefficients relative to that reference, interpolated complex-linearly.
Configuration switches are discontinuities at exact sample indices and are never
interpolated across.

\claim{C-INTERP-BOUND}One target, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}=\numEpsRel$ for $|\Delta
h|/|h|$, sets both the snapshot period and the tile size; half of it goes to each source of
error. The chord error of a path with range $r$ is $k\ddot rT_u^2/8$ with $\ddot r\le
v_\perp^2/r_{\min}$, which gives
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{curv}}=\sqrt{8(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}/2)\,r_{\min}/(k\,v_\perp^2)}.
\label{eq:tcurv}
\end{equation}
The residual of a tile with side $D_t$ turns by $\Delta\phi$, equal to
$2\pi v_{\mathrm{rel}}D_tT_u/(\lambda R_{\min})$, per interval, and the midpoint dip of linear interpolation,
$\Delta\phi^2/8$, must stay below $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}/2$, which bounds $D_t$. The
policy starts from $T_{u0}=\min(T_{\mathrm{curv}},T_{u,\max})$ with $T_{u,\max}$ of
\numTuMaxMs~ms (a scenario can lower $T_u$ but not raise it), evaluates the periods
$T_{u0}/2^m$ for $m$ up to \numTuHalvingsMax{} with the tile counts each needs, and picks
the pair with the lowest estimated compute cost; the choice goes to the manifest.
\claim{C-VERIFY-INTERP}Each run checks the bound: in every \numVerifyStride{}th snapshot
interval the engine evaluates the exact snapshot at the interval midpoint and logs
$\max|h_{\mathrm{interp}}-h_{\mathrm{true}}|/|h|$ per link; the manifest keeps the maximum,
and runs above $\numVerifyFlagFactor\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}$ are flagged (PH-18).
Section~\ref{sec:vv} compares the rendered Doppler shifts of LOS and RIS paths with closed
forms (PH-17).

\subsection{Delay, bandwidth and noise}\label{sec:model-noise}
% channel/interp.py (is_narrowband, plan_groups), channel/fd.py (Kaiser sinc, FD_ROWS,
% N_PHASES polyphase table with linear interpolation, INTEGER_MAX_PHASE_RAD), impair/noise.py
% (thermal_sigma per RX), rng.py (philox4x32-10/blake2b-v1 keys, absolute counters),
% impair/oscillator.py (CFO, Wiener PN), impair/frontend.py.
\claim{C-FD}Each TX stream declares its occupied bandwidth $B$. A link is narrowband when
$\pi B\Delta\tau\le\numNarrowbandRad$~rad over its delay spread $\Delta\tau$; its paths then
share one envelope delay and keep their own exact carrier phases. Otherwise the paths are
grouped so that each group passes the same test, and each group gets its own
fractional-delay (FD) filter at its chord delay. The FD filters are Kaiser-windowed sinc
filters ($\beta=\numKaiserBeta$)~\cite{Laakso1996FracDelay} with \numFdTaps{} taps for
$B/f_s$ up to \numFdBands{}, respectively, applied through a polyphase table of
\numFdPhases{} fractional phases with linear interpolation between neighbouring phases.
Their in-band error stays below \numFdTargetDb~dB for every fractional delay (PH-20); a
wider band is rejected. An integer delay without a filter is used when the band is narrow
enough for the half-sample error, $\pi B/(2f_s)$, to stay below \numIntegerDelayRad~rad.

\claim{C-NOISE}Thermal noise is added once per receiver, not per link: complex Gaussian
noise of power $k_BT_0Ff_s$ with $T_0=\numTzeroK$~K and the receiver noise factor $F$
($k_BT_0$ is \numThermalDbmHz~dBm/Hz) (PH-21). \claim{C-RNG}Every random variate is a pure
function of the master seed, a purpose, a name and an absolute sample or step index: keys
are derived with a BLAKE2b hash of the seed, purpose and name, and the counter-based
Philox4x32-10 generator~\cite{Salmon2011Philox} takes the absolute index as its counter.
Adding a node or chunking a run differently therefore changes no other variate, and the
compiled and reference backends produce identical integer streams (RT-14). Further
impairments are carrier-frequency offsets of TX and RX oscillators, computed as phases of
the absolute sample index (RT-09), Wiener phase noise with keyed increments, and a front end
with full-scale mapping, gain and optional clipping. Table~\ref{tab:modelcard} lists every
component with its validity domain, verification evidence and default.
