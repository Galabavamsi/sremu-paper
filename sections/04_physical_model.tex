\section{Physical Model}\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Scene and snapshots}\label{sec:model-stack}
% physics/geometry.py (Static, Waypoints, ConstantVelocity, RandomWaypoint1D/2D,
% TraceTrajectory, ExternalTrajectory), physics/propagation.py (snapshot, combine; B[r,s,n]
% or separable C[r,n] with gamma[s]; rows = (link, hop variant)), contracts.py conventions.
\claim{C-STACK}Node positions follow analytic trajectories in continuous time. At a snapshot
time the model evaluates, in double precision, every path without a RIS and, per panel, a
coefficient tensor $B[r,s,n]$ over rows $r$ (a link with a ground variant of its two hops),
states $s$ and cells $n$; a configuration selects one state per cell, and $\mathrm{combine}$
forms one path per panel, row and render tile, linearly in $\Gamma$. \claim{C-LOS}The line-of-sight (LOS) path follows the
Friis equation with scalar co-polar antenna patterns and an explicit visibility factor (an
occluder, a partition loss or a coherent knife edge), which never acts on a RIS hop; ground,
order-one walls and a diffuse term are optional (Appendix~\ref{app:model}).

\subsection{RIS kernel K1}\label{sec:model-k1}
% physics/ris_kernel.py (k1_terms, accumulate_cells; obliquity sqrt default, kirchhoff
% option with ValidityWarning, none for includes_cell_pattern; huygens j always on;
% visibility indicator); physics/propagation.py (B tensor phase relative to tile reference).
\claim{C-K1}The RIS contribution of every link is the area-tied aperture kernel K1
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
h_{\mathrm{RIS}}={}&\sum_{q}\sqrt{G_T(\hat r^{i}_q)\,G_R(-\hat r^{s}_q)}\;
  \Gamma_{n(q)}(s,f,\theta_{iq},\theta_{sq})\\
&\times\frac{A_q\,o(\theta_{iq},\theta_{sq})}{4\pi\, r_{iq}\, r_{sq}}\;
  j\,e^{-jk(r_{iq}+r_{sq})}\\
&\times\mathbf{1}[\cos\theta_{iq}>0,\ \cos\theta_{sq}>0],
\end{split}
\label{eq:k1}
\end{equation}
where $q$ runs over quadrature points (one per physical cell, or sub-cells,
Section~\ref{sec:model-cells}) and $n(q)$ is the cell that carries the state and $\Gamma$;
$A_q$ is the pitch area of the quadrature cell, gaps included, so the sub-cell areas of a cell
add up to its pitch area; $r_{iq}$, $r_{sq}$ are the distances from transmitter and receiver to
the point and $\theta_{iq}$, $\theta_{sq}$ the angles toward them from the panel normal
(projected onto one plane for $\Gamma$, Section~\ref{sec:design-states});
$\hat r^i_q$, $\hat r^s_q$ are the unit vectors from transmitter to point and from point to
receiver; $o=\sqrt{\cos\theta_i\cos\theta_s}$ is the default obliquity, the cosine pattern
measured by Tang et al.~\cite{Tang2022mmWave}; $j$ is the Huygens (physical-optics) factor; and
the indicator keeps front-side incidence and reflection, so a path to the back of a panel
contributes exactly zero (PH-09).

\claim{C-K1-AREA}Because each cell's contribution is tied to its area, subdividing a fixed
aperture into more cells leaves the received power unchanged (PH-04,
Section~\ref{sec:vv-tang}), whereas a fixed per-cell gain would grow with every subdivision;
and the factor $j$ sets the phase of the RIS path relative to the LOS path, which would
otherwise be a quarter turn off (PH-05, image theory).
\claim{C-K1-FORMS}For one quadrature point per cell, K1 is algebraically equal to three
published forms: the cell model of Tang et al.~\cite{Tang2022mmWave} times $j$, the general
path-loss model of ETSI GR RIS 003~\cite[clause 6.3.1.2]{ETSI_GR_RIS003_v121} with its
recommended gain $4\pi d_xd_y/\lambda^2$ of a $d_x\times d_y$ cell, and the model of
Ellingson~\cite{Ellingson2021PathLoss} with an element gain tied to the cell area,
$G_e(\psi)=(4\pi A/\lambda^2)\cos\psi$, to double precision (PH-02).

\subsection{Cell model}\label{sec:model-cells}
% physics/quadrature.py (resolve_cell_model, build_quadrature, representative_links,
% richardson_check); scenario/models.py Numerics defaults.
\claim{C-CELL-MODEL}Each panel selects a cell model: \texttt{point}, one quadrature point per
cell, which Tang et al.\ compared with their measurements~\cite{Tang2022mmWave};
\texttt{po\_patch}, sub-cells with sides of at most \numSubcellMax$\lambda$ that share the
cell's $\Gamma$, a uniform-patch model; or \texttt{auto} (default): \texttt{point} for
pitches up to \numPointMaxPitch$\lambda$, else \texttt{po\_patch}. A patch carries a
sub-aperture factor that a point omits: for a half-wavelength cell \numCellRetroADb~dB and
\numCellRetroBDb~dB at \numCellRetroAngleA$^\circ$ and \numCellRetroAngleB$^\circ$
retro-reflection, none at specular reflection (PH-26). Every case study uses \texttt{point},
though the laboratory tiles of \numLabPitchMm~mm lie \numLabPitchMarginMm~mm below the
threshold; forcing \texttt{po\_patch} on them in E-B and E-C moves no loss or switch effect by
more than \numCellSensSwitchMaxDb~dB (Appendix~\ref{app:model}).

\subsection{Element states and non-idealities}\label{sec:model-nonideal}
% physics/nonideal.py (pipeline order, quantize_phases, FailureMap, efficiency_factor,
% CouplingHook without kappa values, binary_md); physics/optimize.py (exact optimum).
\claim{C-NONIDEAL}Each cell's reflection coefficient comes from its response table through a
fixed pipeline: expansion by the panel's control granularity, quantization of continuous
phases by $s=\arg\max_s\mathrm{Re}\{\bar\Gamma(s)e^{-j\phi}\}$, with $\bar\Gamma$ the table at
the panel's reference geometry, correct for unequal amplitudes (PH-30), a failure map, the
table at the cell's angles, an efficiency factor, and a coupling hook, implemented as no
coupling. Mutual coupling is not modelled beyond the locally periodic coupling that a per-state
table taken with identical neighbours contains~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS003_v121}. Closed-form losses of finite alphabets and the mirror and
steerable parts $m$, $d$ of a binary alphabet $\Gamma=m\pm d$ check the pipeline (PH-13,
PH-15, PH-25). \claim{C-OPTIMUM}An exact maximizer of $|z_0+\sum_n Z[s_n,n]|$ over any finite
alphabet, a sweep over sorted critical angles (Appendix~\ref{app:model}), bounds controllers
and studies and matches exhaustive search in every tested instance (PH-24, PH-29); optimal discrete-phase
beamforming has been solved before~\cite{Zhang2022OptimalBinary,Zhao2024OptimalDiscrete}.

\subsection{Mobility, delay and noise}\label{sec:model-doppler}\label{sec:model-noise}
% channel/interp.py (EPS_REL split D1, t_curv_s, tile_side_max_m, choose_policy, M_MAX,
% T_U_MAX_S, VERIFY_INTERP_STRIDE, VERIFY_INTERP_FLAG, verify_interp); engine/scheduler.py
% (snapshot grid, switches never interpolated across); channel/fd.py; impair/noise.py; rng.py.
\claim{C-DOPPLER}Doppler shifts are not parameters: they follow from the geometry. Snapshots
lie on an absolute grid of period $T_u$; between two of them each path without a RIS has a delay
linear in time and the exact carrier phase $e^{-j2\pi f_c\tau_p(t)}$ for that delay, and each
RIS panel row is split into render tiles with a reference delay and an interpolated residual.
Configuration switches are never interpolated across. \claim{C-INTERP-BOUND}One target,
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}=\numEpsRel$ for $|\Delta h|/|h|$, sets both the snapshot period
and the tile size, \claim{C-VERIFY-INTERP}and each run checks it at the midpoint of every
\numVerifyStride{}th snapshot interval (PH-18; Appendix~\ref{app:model}). \claim{C-FD}Paths whose delay spread is too large for one envelope
delay get Kaiser-windowed sinc fractional-delay (FD) filters~\cite{Laakso1996FracDelay} with
an in-band error below \numFdTargetDb~dB (PH-20). \claim{C-NOISE}Thermal noise of power
$k_BT_0Ff_s$, with $T_0=\numTzeroK$~K and the receiver noise factor $F$ ($k_BT_0$ is
\numThermalDbmHz~dBm/Hz), is added once per receiver, not per link (PH-21). \claim{C-RNG}Every
random variate is a keyed pure function of the seed and an absolute sample or step index, so
adding a node or chunking a run changes no other variate (RT-14). Oscillator offsets, phase noise and a
front end with optional clipping complete the impairments; Appendix~\ref{app:model} gives the model card
with the validity domain, evidence and default of every component.
