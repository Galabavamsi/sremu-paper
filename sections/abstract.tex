\begin{abstract}
Controllers for reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) are usually evaluated with ideal
element states, instantaneous reconfiguration and channel coefficients instead of
waveforms, which hides the effect of control timing and hardware states on a live link. We
present sremu, an open-source, software-only emulator that applies RIS-assisted
propagation to complex-baseband samples exchanged with external applications through a
software-defined-radio-style interface. RIS configurations are timestamped commands from an external
controller, each applied at an exact, logged sample on every receiver; per-state element
responses can be imported from measurements. The area-tied kernel reproduces published
RIS path-loss model curves within \numTangCurveMaxDevDb~dB, and a pre-registered
comparison with digitized \numLabFreqGHz~GHz laboratory data \txtVLabOutcome. Lockstep
runs are sample-exact and deterministic; wall-clock-paced runs on \txtPacedPlatform{}
sustain \numPacedLinks{} links at \numPacedMSps~MS/s with a deadline-miss rate below
\numSustainMissRate. Two studies use these semantics: a RIS switch inside a packet
\txtEBResult, and position-driven binary control under signaling delay \txtEAResult.
\end{abstract}
