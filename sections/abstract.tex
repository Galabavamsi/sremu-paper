\begin{abstract}
Controllers of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) are usually tested on per-block
channel coefficients with ideal, instantly switching elements, hiding when a switch lands in
the signal. sremu, a software-only emulator, applies RIS-assisted propagation to
complex-baseband samples exchanged with external applications through a
software-defined-radio-style interface. RIS configurations are timestamped commands from a
controller in the loop, applied at an exact, logged sample on every receiver. In lockstep
sample-level runs with an in-process controller, \numEbGuardMs~ms guards between packets and
\numEbLatencyMs~ms plus up to $J$ of command latency, in a geometry modelled on our
laboratory, \txtEBResult. Headless paced runs with \numBthreeOnePageUs~$\mu$s pages on
\txtPacedPlatform{} sustain \numPacedLinks{} links at \numPacedMSps~MS/s with a deadline-miss
rate below \numSustainMissRate. Against third-party chamber measurements at
\numTangFcGHz~GHz, the emulator's received power is \numTangMeasBiasDb~dB low on average
(RMSE \numTangMeasRmseDb~dB), like the published model it reproduces. On the coefficient path
(within \numVaOneMaxDiffDb~dB of sample-level runs), position-driven binary control under
signaling delay \txtEAResult.
\end{abstract}
