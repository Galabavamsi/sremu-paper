\begin{abstract}
Controllers of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) are usually tested on per-block
channel coefficients with instant switching, hiding when a switch lands in the signal. We
present sremu, a software-only emulator that applies RIS-assisted propagation to
complex-baseband (IQ) samples of external applications, such as unmodified GNU Radio, behind a
software-defined-radio-style interface, and executes timestamped commands of a controller in
the loop at an exact, logged sample on every receiver. In lockstep runs with modelled command
latency and the line of sight blocked, the IQ-level packet error rate follows the closed-form
probability that a switch lands after the preamble; with the line of sight present,
\numEbLosLoExposedMinPct--\numEbLosLoExposedMaxPct~\% of such packets fail at
$E_s/N_0=\numEbArcEsnoLowDb$~dB. Headless paced runs with \numBthreeOnePageUs~$\mu$s pages on
\txtPacedPlatform{} sustain \numPacedLinks{} links at \numPacedMSps~MS/s with a deadline-miss
rate below \numSustainMissRate. On an offline evaluator of channel coefficients,
position-driven binary control loses half its benefit after a signaling delay of
\numEaTauLambdaVMin--\numEaTauLambdaVMax\,$\lambda/v$ over \numEaGeometries{} geometries
(\numEaTauOverLambdaV\,$\lambda/v$ in the primary one; $\lambda$: wavelength, $v$: speed). The
emulator reproduces a published path-loss model, inheriting its \numTangPubMeasBiasDb~dB mean
offset from third-party chamber measurements at \numTangFcGHz~GHz; no switching or timing
behaviour was compared with measurements.
\end{abstract}
