\begin{abstract}
Controllers of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) are usually tested on per-block
channel coefficients, blind to when a switch lands in the signal. We present sremu, a
software-only emulator that applies RIS-assisted propagation to complex-baseband (IQ) samples
of external applications and executes timestamped commands of an in-the-loop controller at an
exact, logged sample on every receiver. In lockstep runs with modelled latency and a blocked
line of sight, the IQ-level packet error rate follows the closed-form probability that a
switch lands after the preamble; unblocked,
\numEbLosLoExposedMinPct--\numEbLosLoExposedMaxPct~\% of such packets fail at
$E_s/N_0=\numEbArcEsnoLowDb$~dB. On \txtPacedPlatform{}, sremu alone (headless) sustains
\numPacedLinks{} transmitter--receiver links at \numPacedMSps~MS/s, missing under
\numSustainMissRate{} of \numSustainSlackMs~ms page deadlines; with \numBfourLinks{} links
serving an external controller's \numBfourRateLowHz--\numBfourRateMidHz~commands/s, it applied
at least
\numBfourExactOkMinPct~\% of commands timed \numBfourLeadOkMs~ms ahead at their exact sample,
but missed \numBfourMissLowMinPct--\numBfourMissMidMaxPct~\% of these deadlines. With an
offline coefficient evaluator, position-driven binary control loses half its benefit after
\numEaTauLambdaVMin--\numEaTauLambdaVMax\,$\lambda/v$ of signaling delay (wavelength over
speed) over \numEaGeometries{} geometries. The emulator reproduces a published
path-loss model, inheriting its \numTangPubMeasBiasDb~dB mean offset from third-party
\numTangFcGHz~GHz chamber measurements; no switching or timing behaviour was compared with
measurements.
\end{abstract}
