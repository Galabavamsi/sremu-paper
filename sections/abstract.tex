\begin{abstract}
Controllers for reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) are usually evaluated with ideal
element states, instantaneous reconfiguration and channel coefficients instead of
waveforms, which hides how control timing and hardware states affect a live link. We
present sremu, an open-source, software-only emulator that applies RIS-assisted
propagation to complex-baseband samples exchanged with external applications through a
software-defined-radio-style interface. RIS configurations are timestamped commands from an
external controller, each applied at an exact, logged sample on every receiver; per-state
element responses can be imported from measurements. The area-tied kernel reproduces
published RIS path-loss model curves within \numTangCurveMaxDevDb~dB, and a pre-registered
comparison with a digitized \numLabFreqGHz~GHz laboratory measurement \txtVLabOutcome.
Lockstep runs are sample-exact and deterministic; wall-clock-paced runs on
\txtPacedPlatform{} sustain \numPacedLinks{} links at \numPacedMSps~MS/s with a
deadline-miss rate below \numSustainMissRate. In a simulated geometry modelled on our
laboratory, with RIS switches in \numEbGuardMs~ms guards between packets, \txtEBResult{}
when mistimed switches cost bits; position-driven binary control under signaling delay
\txtEAResult.
\end{abstract}
