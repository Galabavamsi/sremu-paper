\section{Case Studies}\label{sec:cases}

The studies below use the semantics of Section~\ref{sec:design}. Their geometries are
simulated (SIM) and labelled, and every assumed input is named. E-B depends on when a
command takes effect within the sample stream and runs at the sample level. E-A depends on how
old the information behind a command is; it runs on the batch path, with a consistency check
at the sample level. E-C and E-D are short analyses on the batch path, and the last
subsection describes runs with external applications.

\subsection{Intra-frame RIS reconfiguration}\label{sec:cases-eb}
\claim{C-EB-WHY}A tool that works with one channel coefficient per block cannot place a
reconfiguration inside a packet and observe the symbols on either side of it. ETSI GS RIS
008 lists reconfiguration time and modulated-signal quality among the parameters of a RIS
test~\cite{ETSI_GS_RIS008}, and ETSI GR RIS 004 sizes the delay budget of a RIS controller
by the frame structure of 5G NR~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS004}; this study measures how a switch inside
a packet and the timing of the command behind it interact. It runs at the sample level, in
lockstep with an in-process controller, so the command latency is modelled rather than
measured. Three lessons stand out: an equalizer trained before a switch inside a packet sees
an error as large as the signal after it; a long linear transition spreads the damage but
does not remove it; and with a strong line of sight QPSK tolerates the wrong configuration,
so the timing of a command matters where the RIS path dominates or the margin is small.

\claim{C-EB-SETUP}The primary geometry is modelled on the laboratory arrangement of
Section~\ref{sec:vv-lab}. It is simulated (SIM) with nominal dimensions: each uncertain distance
and angle takes the middle of its known range. It is therefore not
the measured geometry of the laboratory, and no measurement is compared with these runs. The
\txtEbArcPanels{} tiles of $\numEbArcCellsU\times\numEbArcCellsV$ cells stand on an arc of
radius \numEbArcRadiusM~m around the transmitter and act as one surface at \numEbFcGHz~GHz.
Two receivers stand \numEbArcRxDistM~m from the transmitter, at different angles; their
positions are an engineering choice (ENG). Each
receiver has its own exact optimum over all cells, and a switch sends one command per tile,
all for the same time. A lab-like SIM geometry with one panel of three tiles is a supporting
case. Three assumptions apply to both geometries. The two states of the laboratory cells have
unit amplitude ($|\Gamma|=1$, ASSUMED), because their magnitudes were not measured. During a
transition the response follows the complex-linear path between the states, an engineering
choice (ENG). When the line of sight is blocked, the occluder removes the direct path and leaves
the RIS hops unattenuated; on the arc a real plate of that size would also shadow some RIS
rays, so this case is an idealization. QPSK packets of \numEbPreamble{} preamble and
\numEbPayload{} payload symbols, at \numEbSps{} samples per symbol and \numEbRateMsps~MS/s,
last \numEbPacketMs~ms. The receiver equalizes once, from the preamble. The transmit power
gives $E_s/N_0=\numEbEsnoDb$~dB at RX1 against the physical noise.

\claim{C-EB-MECH}In the mechanism experiment (E-B1) the surface switches to the
configuration of RX2 during a packet to RX1. VA-5 checks every step switch inside a packet:
\numVaFiveArcTotal{} on the arc and \numVaFiveTotal{} in the lab-like geometry. In all of
them the earliest affected sample of RX1 is exactly
$\mathrm{to\_ticks}(\tsw+\tau_{\mathrm{RIS}\to\mathrm{RX1}})$, and every earlier sample is
identical bit for bit to the run without a switch. On the arc, the command of each tile
takes effect at the sample set by that tile's own delay to RX1. Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(a) shows
the effect on the arc. With the line of sight blocked, the switch changes the channel of RX1
by \numEbArcBlockedChangeDb~dB and \numEbArcBlockedRotDeg$^\circ$. Symbols before it keep
their EVM of about \numEbArcRefEvmDb~dB. After it the equalizer, trained on the old channel,
sees an error as large as the signal: \numEbArcOneErrors{} of \numEbOneBits{} payload bits
fail when the switch comes a quarter into the packet. A linear transition of
\numEbTransLongUs~$\mu$s spreads the damage over more symbols but still costs
\numEbArcLinErrors{} bits. With the line of sight present, the switch changes the channel by
\numEbArcLosChangeDb~dB and \numEbArcLosRotDeg$^\circ$. The payload EVM rises to
\numEbArcLosEvmDb~dB, and the packet loses \numEbArcLosOneErrors{} of its \numEbOneBits{}
payload bits.

\claim{C-EB-TIMING}In the timing experiment (E-B2) \numEbPackets{} packets alternate between
the two receivers; the slice shown uses guard intervals of \numEbGuardMs~ms. A controller
sends one message per guard, with one command per tile on the arc, and the message arrives
after a modelled latency of \numEbLatencyMs~ms plus a uniform jitter of up to $J$. Timed
commands carry a switch time at the guard centre and are issued \numEbLeadsMs~ms ahead; a
timed command that arrives late applies on arrival. Commands without a time apply on arrival
(asap), as untimed UHD commands do~\cite{UHDManual}. The arc run fixed its cases before any
point ran: the line of sight present at $E_s/N_0=\numEbEsnoDb$~dB, the same at
\numEbArcEsnoLowDb~dB as a sensitivity, and the line of sight blocked. With the line of sight
present at \numEbEsnoDb~dB, QPSK tolerates the wrong configuration: in this slice no
semantics loses more than \numEbArcLosHighPerMaxPct~\% of the packets, although the EVM of the
affected packets rises. Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(b) shows the other two cases, in which a switch at
the wrong time costs bits; each pair of values below gives the line of sight present at
\numEbArcEsnoLowDb~dB, then the line of sight blocked. Asap commands lose no packet while the
worst latency fits in the guard ($J\le\numEbArcAsapJitterOkMs$~ms), and they lose
\numEbArcAsapPerOneLosPct~\% and \numEbArcAsapPerOneBlkPct~\% at $J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms.
Timed commands with a lead of \numEbLeadMidMs~ms lose no packet up to $J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms
(Clopper--Pearson upper limit \numEbPerUpperPct~\%). At $J=\numEbJitterMaxMs$~ms the latency
can exceed this lead, and they lose \numEbArcTimedTwoPerThreeLosPct~\% and
\numEbArcTimedTwoPerThreeBlkPct~\%. With a lead of \numEbLeadMinMs~ms they fail already at
$J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms (\numEbArcTimedHalfPerOneLosPct~\% and
\numEbArcTimedHalfPerOneBlkPct~\%); with \numEbLeadMaxMs~ms they lose no packet at any jitter.
That a timed command loses no packet when its lead exceeds the worst latency, and a command
without a time none while the worst latency fits in the guard, is the designed behaviour of
the command semantics, which E-B2 verifies on IQ samples. What the experiment adds is how much
a mistimed switch costs (E-B1) and how fast the loss grows once the latency leaves the guard
or exceeds the lead.

\claim{C-EB-TICK}A third semantics models a controller that latches commands on its own
free-running timer: each command applies at the next tick of a \numEbTickMs~ms clock that
starts at $t=0$, is not aligned with the packets and keeps one pending command per panel.
Published RIS experiments with a 5G NR testbed switch the surface on such a timer, without
coordination with the base station~\cite{Yashvanth2026Random,Kiran2026Random}. The analogy
lies in the effect, a switch at an arbitrary point of a packet; there the switching period is
seconds, whereas here it is comparable to a packet. In both cases of Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(b) the
tick-based semantics lose \numEbArcTickPerMinPct--\numEbArcTickPerMaxPct~\% of the packets at
every jitter, also without jitter: with a timer period of the order of a packet, alignment
with the frame decides, not the speed of the controller.

\claim{C-EB-SERVED}These runs are lockstep with a modelled latency. Served over the control
socket in paced mode (Section~\ref{sec:wallclock}), an asap command became visible after a
median of \numBfourVisPfiftyMinMs--\numBfourVisPfiftyMaxMs~ms, with p99
\numBfourVisPninetynineMinMs--\numBfourVisPninetynineMaxMs~ms and rare outliers of up to
\numBfourVisMaxMs~ms, and with the \numBfourLeadMs~ms lead used there
\numBfourLateRepMinPct{} to \numBfourLateRepMaxPct~\% of the timed commands per repetition
were late at \numBfourRateLowHz~commands/s. On that path a timed command is on time when its
lead exceeds the command latency plus the error of the client's estimate of virtual time
(Section~\ref{sec:wallclock}), and the regime $J\le\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms of Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}
is not yet reachable through the served path.

\claim{C-EB-LABLIKE}The lab-like geometry supports this picture (Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(c),
(d)). With the line of sight blocked, the switch changes the channel of RX1 by
\numEbBlockedChangeDb~dB and \numEbBlockedRotDeg$^\circ$, and \numEbOneErrors{} payload bits
fail for the same switch time. Timed commands with the \numEbLeadMidMs~ms lead lose no packet
up to $J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms and \numEbTimedTwoPerThreePct~\% at $J=\numEbJitterMaxMs$~ms.
Asap commands lose \numEbAsapPerOnePct~\% at $J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms. Tick-based semantics
lose \numEbTickPerZeroPct~\% without jitter and up to \numEbTickPerMaxPct~\% with it. With the
line of sight present, no semantics loses a packet at \numEbEsnoDb~dB; this geometry was not
run at \numEbArcEsnoLowDb~dB.

\subsection{Signaling delay in closed-loop binary RIS control}\label{sec:cases-ea}
\claim{C-EA-SETUP}A user walks a corridor of \numEaLengthM~m as a one-dimensional random
waypoint at \numEaSpeed~m/s. A line of on--off cells runs parallel to it at
\numEaHeightM~m (\numEaCellsOne{} cells at \numEaFcOneGHz~GHz, \numEaCellsTwo{} at
\numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz). The access point sits \numEaApOffsetM~m off the user line, which keeps
the line-of-sight power finite everywhere on the line. A partition attenuates the line of
sight, not the RIS hops, by \numEaLossDb~dB, which gives the RIS room to help. Position-driven
controllers act on position reports that are $\tau_{\mathrm{SUI}}$ old: the exact optimum and
our reading of the heuristic of~\cite{Mishra2025WCL}. Two controllers need no position: uniform
random activation (URAP) and a bandit that probes a codebook of exact optima and receives a
power report once per $\tau_{\mathrm{SUI}}$. Each point runs
on the batch path for \numEaBatches{} batches of \numEaPeriods{} mean movement periods. The
hypotheses and their ranges were in the emulator's design document before the sweep: GitHub's
push log (Section~\ref{sec:vv-lab}) records it in the initial push of the emulator repository.
The plan that adds the decision rules and the evaluation rule (SHA-256 \txtEaPlanSha\ldots{}
with LF line ends) was written before the sweep by the authors' record, but committed to the
paper repository after it (commit \txtEaPlanCommit), so no third party dates those parts. One
amendment, made before the full sweep after a short IQ run showed that evaluating each time
step at its start overestimates the power, moved the evaluation to the step's midpoint; the
plan discloses it and a short pipeline test seen before it.

\claim{C-EA-RESULT}With instantaneous position knowledge the exact optimum gains
\numEaGainOpt~dB over RIS absent at \numEaFcOneGHz~GHz (\numCiPct\,\% interval
\numEaGainOptLo--\numEaGainOptHi~dB, mean of the per-batch dB gains), and \numEaGainOptTwo~dB
at \numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz. Fig.~\ref{fig:F10} shows how the gain decays with the signaling delay.
We measure the decay by the retention $\rho(\tau)=(P(\tau)-P_{\mathrm{stale}})/(P(0)-
P_{\mathrm{stale}})$ in linear power, where $P_{\mathrm{stale}}$ uses a configuration optimal
for an unrelated position. Half the benefit is lost at
$\tau_{1/2}=\numEaTauHalf$~s (\numCiPct\,\% interval \numEaTauHalfLo--\numEaTauHalfHi~s),
\numEaPeriodOverTau{} times shorter than the mean movement period, and at \numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz
at $\tau_{1/2}=\numEaTauHalfTwo$~s. In units of $\lambda/v$ the two half-lives are
\numEaTauOverLambdaV{} and \numEaTauOverLambdaVTwo: the time scale is $\lambda/v$, the
classical Doppler scaling of channel decorrelation for a moving
terminal~\cite{Clarke1968,Jakes1974}, not the mobility period, and outdated channel knowledge
is known to erode RIS gains under
mobility~\cite{Papazafeiropoulos2023Aging,Zhang2022OutdatedCSI}. The constant belongs to this
geometry and metric. For isotropic scattering the field and envelope correlations fall to one
half after \numClarkeFieldHalf{} and \numClarkeEnvHalf{} $\lambda/v$~\cite{Clarke1968}. The
rule-of-thumb coherence time $\sqrt{9/(16\pi)}/f_D=\numCoherenceRuleLambdaV\,\lambda/v$ lies
close to the E-A constant, but it is the geometric mean of $1/f_D$ and the envelope
half-correlation time, not a correlation level. The ratio of the two half-lives, \numEaRatio{}
(\numEaRatioLo--\numEaRatioHi), lies \numEaRatioOffsetPct~\% above the wavelength ratio
\numEaRatioPred; the corridor is the same in metres at both carriers, so it is not the same
geometry in wavelengths and exact scaling is not expected. Both intervals lie inside the ranges
fixed before the sweep, so H-A1 and H-A2 are supported, but the ranges were wide
(\numEaHaOneLoS--\numEaHaOneHiS~s for H-A1, \numEaHaTwoLo--\numEaHaTwoHi{} for H-A2), so the
support is a weak test. What E-A adds to the classical scaling is the constant for this
geometry, the retention metric and the result for controllers without positions: the bandit
beats URAP at every delay from \numEaTauBanditMinS~s by \numEaBanditMinDb--%
\numEaBanditMaxDb~dB (H-A3 supported), but it stays near the stale level
(\numEaGainBandit~dB against \numEaGainStale~dB), and URAP gains \numEaGainUrap~dB. Our reading
of the heuristic of~\cite{Mishra2025WCL} reaches \numEaGainWclFast~dB at the shortest delay. A
position-driven controller pays for its signaling link when its position reports arrive
before the user has moved a fraction of a wavelength.

\claim{C-VA1}As a consistency check, not a finding, \numVaOneRows{} runs of
\numVaOneDurS~s went through the served engine with an external controller process in
barrier mode and an external receiver application. They agree with the batch evaluation of
E-A within \numVaOneMaxDiffDb~dB, inside the pre-specified limit of \numVaOneTolDb~dB (VA-1,
Fig.~\ref{fig:F10}(c)); the engine's batch path, which evaluates each step at its start, reads
up to \numVaOneLeftMaxDb~dB higher than these runs.

\claim{C-EC}Because the optimum over a finite alphabet is exact
(Section~\ref{sec:model-nonideal}), the cost of a non-ideality is a short analytic result
rather than the outcome of a heuristic. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:F11} list
the loss of the optimized RIS term relative to ideal continuous phase in four settings. The
results agree with prior work on discrete and phase-dependent
states~\cite{WuZhang2020Discrete,Abeywickrama2020Practical}. One-bit control costs
\numEcOneBitMin--\numEcOneBitMax~dB, less than the large-$N$ closed-form loss of
\numEcOneBitEta~dB, because a finite panel in a fixed geometry is not a random phase
ensemble. The laboratory states, with magnitudes ASSUMED to be unit
(Section~\ref{sec:cases-eb}), are nearly antipodal: in $\Gamma=m+ds$ the steerable part
$|d|$ is \numEcAbsD~dB and the mirror part $|m|$ is \numEcAbsM~dB, so they behave like ideal
one-bit states (within \numEcLabVsOneBitMaxDb~dB). Unequal amplitudes cost more, up to
\numEcHalfMaxDb~dB when one state reflects at half amplitude, and an efficiency of one half
adds about \numEcEffExtraDb~dB. Designing for an ideal alphabet and deploying on the true
states costs \numEcDesignPenaltyMinDb--\numEcDesignPenaltyMaxDb~dB more than optimizing for
the true states.

\subsection{FM-band dimensioning}\label{sec:cases-ed}
\claim{C-ED}This subsection is model-based dimensioning, not validated. Can a focused RIS
restore an FM broadcast link of \numFmLinkKm~km at \numFmFreqMHz~MHz behind an obstruction?
With a flat-earth two-ray direct path, a coherent knife edge and ground on both RIS hops, the
aperture needed for a RIS path as strong as the direct path is \numFmAreqMin--\numFmAreqMax~m$^2$
over the receiver heights and distances of Fig.~\ref{fig:F12}. At grazing obstruction, over the
same heights and distances, the RIS path of a \numFmPanelMtwo~m$^2$ panel is
\numFmGapMinDb--\numFmGapMaxDb~dB weaker than the direct path. Over all map entries with this direct path, for two placements of the panel and
with or without ground on its hops, the panel suffices in \numFmPrimarySufficient{} of
\numFmPrimaryEntries{}. A four-ray direct path over flat ground, a model-form sensitivity,
gives \numFmFourRaySufficient{} entries. The picture changes with the direct-path model: with the
experimental ITU-R P.526 smooth-earth path of \sremu{}, which is weaker, the same panel suffices in
\numFmPfiveSufficient{} of \numFmPfiveEntries{} entries. That module is not validated, so the
dimensioning question is decided by the direct-path model rather than by the RIS.

\subsection{Integration with external IQ applications}\label{sec:cases-ee}

\claim{C-EE}Two of the studies above already ran with external processes: the frequency-offset
sweep between two processes over the native data plane (V-CFO-3) and the consistency runs of
E-A with an external controller and receiver (VA-1). The raw request--reply mode
(Section~\ref{sec:design-if}) ran with unmodified GNU Radio \txtEeGrVersion{} (Ubuntu package)
as the peer, in a separate process that knows nothing of the emulator: its stock ZMQ request
and reply blocks and a DQPSK modem built from stock blocks, with \numEeSps{} samples per
symbol and blind timing and carrier recovery. In the scene (SIM), transmitter and receiver stand
\numEeDistM~m from an ideal 1-bit RIS of \numEeCells{} cells at \numEeFcGHz~GHz, a partition
attenuates the direct path by \numEeWallDb~dB, and the sample rate is \numEeRateMsps~MS/s.

\claim{C-EE-LINK}Each of \numEeConfigs{} RIS configurations ran for \numEeStaticS~s of virtual
time as a fresh run with a fresh modem (Table~\ref{tab:ee}). GNU Radio makes no symbol error
with focusing; its symbol error rate (SER) and its error rate of uncoded 200-bit blocks do not
decrease as the gain of the configuration falls, and the random configuration loses every
block. The gains relative to focusing lie within \numEeAfMaxDiffDb~dB of an isotropic
array-factor prediction, an internal consistency check of the emulator, not a measured RIS
gain. Where at least one hundred symbol errors were counted with the link up, the SER implies
an implementation loss of \numEeLossMinDb--\numEeLossMaxDb~dB against coherent DQPSK theory at
the estimated $E_s/N_0$; with the link lost, the implied loss is larger.

\claim{C-EE-TIMING}A further run switched the RIS three times, \numEeSegmentS~s apart, by
commands scheduled over the control socket before virtual time started. Each switch was
applied with zero lateness, and the maximum-likelihood change point of the IQ that GNU Radio
received lies at the logged switch sample (largest offset \numEeCpOffsetMax{} samples). In
every run GNU Radio received every sample, bit for bit the receive stream that the emulator
recorded itself, and every engine and transport counter stayed zero. Across \txtEeReps{}
independent repetitions of all \numEeRuns{} runs, the SHA-256 digests of the received IQ and
of GNU Radio's decisions are identical.

\claim{C-EE-SPEED}These runs are lockstep, so their time is virtual: the sample exchange with
GNU Radio paces the emulator, and the wall-clock speed changes how long a run takes, not its
samples. In \txtEeLaptopSweeps{} sweeps of single runs on a laptop (\txtEeLaptopCpu{}, WSL2,
host load not controlled), the loop advanced at an RTF of
\numEeLaptopRtfLoMin--\numEeLaptopRtfLoMax{} at \numEeRateMsps~MS/s and
\numEeLaptopRtfHiMin--\numEeLaptopRtfHiMax{} at \numEeLaptopRateHiMsps~MS/s; on an AWS
\txtEeAwsInstance{} instance it reached \numEeAwsRtfLo{} at \numEeRateMsps~MS/s.

\claim{C-EE-NOTRUN}srsRAN and OpenAirInterface were analysed but not run. srsRAN~4G pads its
transmit stream to the end of the receive block it is about to read and no further, while
lockstep needs the transmit stream one fractional-delay look-ahead beyond the received
samples; a lockstep srsRAN pair therefore needs a per-peer transmit delay and partial receive
replies, and srsRAN~4G has no package for the Ubuntu release used here. The OpenAirInterface
RF simulator would need a new transport for its own TCP framing~\cite{OAIrfsim}. Both are
future work.
