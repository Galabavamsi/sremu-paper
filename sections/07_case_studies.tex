\section{Case Studies}\label{sec:cases}

The four studies below use the semantics of Section~\ref{sec:design}. Their geometries are
simulated (SIM) and labelled, and every assumed input is named. E-B depends on when a
command takes effect within the sample stream. E-A depends on how old the information behind
a command is; it runs on the batch path, with a consistency check at the sample level. E-C
and E-D are short analyses on the batch path.

\subsection{Intra-frame RIS reconfiguration}\label{sec:cases-eb}
\claim{C-EB-WHY}A tool that works with one channel coefficient per block cannot place a
reconfiguration inside a packet and observe the symbols on either side of it. ETSI GS RIS
008 lists reconfiguration time and modulated-signal quality among the parameters of a RIS
test~\cite{ETSI_GS_RIS008}; this study measures how the two interact. A panel of three tiles
serves two receivers at \numEbFcGHz~GHz in a lab-like SIM geometry, not the laboratory's
geometry. Each receiver has its own exact optimum, and the two states have unit amplitude by
assumption. QPSK packets of \numEbPreamble{} preamble and \numEbPayload{} payload symbols, at
\numEbSps{} samples per symbol and \numEbRateMsps~MS/s, last \numEbPacketMs~ms. The receiver
equalizes once, from the preamble. The transmit power gives $E_s/N_0=\numEbEsnoDb$~dB at
RX1 against the physical noise.

\claim{C-EB-MECH}In the mechanism experiment (E-B1) the panel switches to the
configuration of RX2 during a packet to RX1. In all \numVaFiveTotal{} step switches inside a
packet, the earliest affected sample of RX1 is exactly
$\mathrm{to\_ticks}(\tsw+\tau_{\mathrm{RIS}\to\mathrm{RX1}})$, and every earlier sample is identical bit
for bit to the run without a switch (VA-5). Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(a) shows the effect with the
line of sight blocked. The switch changes the channel of RX1 by
\numEbBlockedChangeDb~dB and \numEbBlockedRotDeg$^\circ$. Symbols before it keep their EVM of
about \numEbRefEvmDb~dB. After it the equalizer, trained on the old channel, sees an error as
large as the signal: \numEbOneErrors{} of \numEbOneBits{} payload bits fail when the switch
comes a quarter into the packet. A linear transition of \numEbTransLongUs~$\mu$s spreads the
damage over more symbols but still costs \numEbLinErrors{} bits. With the line of sight
present the same switch changes the channel by \numEbLosChangeDb~dB and
\numEbLosRotDeg$^\circ$, so no bit fails, but the payload EVM rises to \numEbLosEvmDb~dB.

\claim{C-EB-TIMING}In the timing experiment (E-B2) \numEbPackets{} packets alternate between the
two receivers; the slice shown uses guard intervals of \numEbGuardMs~ms. A controller sends one command per
guard, and the command arrives after \numEbLatencyMs~ms plus a uniform jitter of up to $J$.
The study design makes the case with the line of sight primary and blocking a declared
sensitivity variant. With the line of sight present, no semantics loses a packet at this SNR
(largest packet error rate \numEbLosPerMaxPct~\%), although the EVM of the affected packets
rises. Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(b) therefore shows the blocked variant, where the RIS path dominates
and the semantics differ.
Timed commands carry a switch time at the guard centre and are issued \numEbLeadsMs~ms ahead.
Commands without a time apply on arrival (asap). Tick-based untimed semantics apply each
command at the next tick of a \numEbTickMs~ms grid and keep one pending command per panel.
Timed commands lose no packet as long as the lead exceeds the latency: with a lead of
\numEbLeadMaxMs~ms the packet error rate is zero up to $J=\numEbJitterMaxMs$~ms
(Clopper--Pearson upper limit \numEbPerUpperPct~\%). A short lead fails when the jitter
outgrows it (\numEbTimedHalfPerOnePct~\% at $J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms with a lead of \numEbLeadMinMs~ms). Asap
commands fail when the latency outgrows the guard (\numEbAsapPerOnePct~\% at $J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms).
Tick-based semantics lose \numEbTickPerZeroPct~\% of the packets even without jitter, because
the tick grid is not aligned with the guards, and up to \numEbTickPerMaxPct~\% with it. When
the RIS path dominates, the timing requirement is therefore simple to state and to test: a
command must carry a time, and its lead must exceed the worst latency. A run on the as-built arc geometry of the laboratory panel is
pending; its result will be stated as \txtEBArcResult.

\subsection{Signaling delay in closed-loop binary RIS control}\label{sec:cases-ea}
\claim{C-EA-SETUP}A user walks a corridor of \numEaLengthM~m as a one-dimensional random
waypoint at \numEaSpeed~m/s. A line of on--off cells runs parallel to it at
\numEaHeightM~m (\numEaCellsOne{} cells at \numEaFcOneGHz~GHz, \numEaCellsTwo{} at
\numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz). The access point sits \numEaApOffsetM~m off the user line, a documented
deviation that keeps the mean line-of-sight power finite. A partition attenuates the line of
sight, not the RIS hops, by \numEaLossDb~dB, which gives the RIS room to help; in the
unblocked corridor of the original study~\cite{Mishra2025WCL} the exact optimum gains
\numEaGainAzero~dB under the area-tied kernel. Position-driven
controllers act on position reports that are $\tau_{\mathrm{SUI}}$ old: the exact optimum and
our reading of the heuristic of~\cite{Mishra2025WCL}. Two controllers need no position: uniform
random activation (URAP) and a bandit that probes a codebook of exact optima and receives a
power report once per $\tau_{\mathrm{SUI}}$. Each point runs
on the batch path for \numEaBatches{} batches of \numEaPeriods{} mean movement periods. The
hypotheses, the evaluation rule and the decision rules were written before the sweep
(\texttt{paper/preregistration/E-A.md}). One amendment, made before the full sweep, changed
the evaluation of each time step from its start to its midpoint; a short pipeline test seen
before the amendment is disclosed there.

\claim{C-EA-RESULT}With instantaneous position knowledge the exact optimum gains
\numEaGainOpt~dB over RIS absent at \numEaFcOneGHz~GHz (\numCiPct\,\% interval
\numEaGainOptLo--\numEaGainOptHi~dB, mean of the per-batch dB gains), and \numEaGainOptTwo~dB
at \numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz. Fig.~\ref{fig:F10} shows how the gain decays with the signaling delay.
We measure the decay by the retention $\rho(\tau)=(P(\tau)-P_{\mathrm{stale}})/(P(0)-
P_{\mathrm{stale}})$ in linear power, where $P_{\mathrm{stale}}$ uses a configuration optimal
for an unrelated position. Half the benefit is lost at
$\tau_{1/2}=\numEaTauHalf$~s (\numCiPct\,\% interval \numEaTauHalfLo--\numEaTauHalfHi~s),
\numEaPeriodOverTau{} times shorter than the mean movement period. The interval lies inside the
range fixed before the sweep, so hypothesis H-A1 is supported. At \numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz, $\tau_{1/2}=\numEaTauHalfTwo$~s; the ratio of the two,
\numEaRatio{} (\numEaRatioLo--\numEaRatioHi), is close to the ratio of the wavelengths,
\numEaRatioPred, and its interval lies inside the range fixed before the sweep, so H-A2 is
supported: the time scale is set by $\lambda/v$
($\tau_{1/2}\approx\numEaTauOverLambdaV\,\lambda/v$ here), not by the mobility period. The
bandit beats URAP at every delay from \numEaTauBanditMinS~s by \numEaBanditMinDb--%
\numEaBanditMaxDb~dB (H-A3 supported), but it stays near the stale level
(\numEaGainBandit~dB against \numEaGainStale~dB), and URAP gains \numEaGainUrap~dB. A
position-driven controller pays for its signaling link when its position reports arrive
before the user has moved a fraction of a wavelength.

\claim{C-VA1}As a consistency check, not a finding, \numVaOneRows{} runs of
\numVaOneDurS~s went through the served engine with an external controller process in
barrier mode and an external receiver application. They agree with the batch path within
\numVaOneMaxDiffDb~dB, inside the pre-set limit of \numVaOneTolDb~dB (VA-1,
Fig.~\ref{fig:F10}(c)).

\claim{C-EC}Because the optimum over a finite alphabet is exact
(Section~\ref{sec:model-nonideal}), the cost of a non-ideality is a short analytic result
rather than the outcome of a heuristic. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:F11} list
the loss of the optimized RIS term relative to ideal continuous phase in four settings. The
results agree with prior work on discrete and phase-dependent
states~\cite{WuZhang2020Discrete,Abeywickrama2020Practical}. One-bit control costs
\numEcOneBitMin--\numEcOneBitMax~dB, less than the large-$N$ closed-form loss of
\numEcOneBitEta~dB, because a finite panel in a fixed geometry is not a random phase
ensemble. The laboratory states are nearly antipodal: in $\Gamma=m+ds$ the steerable part
$|d|$ is \numEcAbsD~dB and the mirror part $|m|$ is \numEcAbsM~dB, so they behave like ideal
one-bit states (within \numEcLabVsOneBitMaxDb~dB). Unequal amplitudes cost more, up to
\numEcHalfMaxDb~dB when one state reflects at half amplitude, and an efficiency of one half
adds about \numEcEffExtraDb~dB. Designing for an ideal alphabet and deploying on the true
states costs \numEcDesignPenaltyMinDb--\numEcDesignPenaltyMaxDb~dB more than optimizing for
the true states.

\subsection{FM-band dimensioning}\label{sec:cases-ed}
\claim{C-ED}This subsection is model-based dimensioning, not validated. Can a focused RIS
restore an FM broadcast link of \numFmLinkKm~km at \numFmFreqMHz~MHz behind an obstruction?
With a flat-earth two-ray direct path, a coherent knife edge and ground on both RIS hops, the
aperture needed for a RIS path as strong as the direct path is \numFmAreqMin--\numFmAreqMax~m$^2$
over the receiver heights and distances of Fig.~\ref{fig:F12}. At grazing obstruction, over the
same heights and distances, the RIS path of a \numFmPanelMtwo~m$^2$ panel is
\numFmGapMinDb--\numFmGapMaxDb~dB weaker than the direct path. Over all map entries with this direct path, for two placements of the panel and
with or without ground on its hops, the panel suffices in \numFmPrimarySufficient{} of
\numFmPrimaryEntries{}. A four-ray direct path over flat ground, a model-form sensitivity,
gives \numFmFourRaySufficient{} entries. The picture changes with the direct-path model: with the
experimental ITU-R P.526 smooth-earth path of \sremu{}, which is weaker, the same panel suffices in
\numFmPfiveSufficient{} of \numFmPfiveEntries{} entries. That module is not validated, so the
dimensioning question is decided by the direct-path model rather than by the RIS.
