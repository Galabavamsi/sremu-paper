\section{Case Studies}\label{sec:cases}

The studies use the semantics of Section~\ref{sec:design}; their geometries are simulated
(SIM) and labelled, and every assumed input is named. E-B depends on when a command takes
effect and runs at the sample level; E-A depends on how old the information behind a command
is and is evaluated offline on channel coefficients, with a sample-level consistency check; E-C
evaluates static channel coefficients; E-E runs unmodified radio software. Appendix~\ref{app:cases} adds details and a
model-based FM-band dimensioning (E-D).

\subsection{Intra-frame RIS reconfiguration}\label{sec:cases-eb}
\claim{C-EB-WHY}A tool with one channel coefficient per block cannot place a reconfiguration
inside a packet. ETSI GS RIS 008 lists reconfiguration time and modulated-signal quality among
the parameters of a RIS test~\cite{ETSI_GS_RIS008}, and ETSI GR RIS 004 sizes the delay budget
of a RIS controller by the frame structure of 5G NR~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS004}; this study measures
how a switch inside a packet and the timing of the command behind it interact, at the sample
level and in lockstep with an in-process controller, so the command latency is modelled.

\claim{C-EB-SETUP}The geometry is modelled on the laboratory arrangement of
Section~\ref{sec:vv-lab} but simulated (SIM) with nominal dimensions (the middle of each known
range); it is not the measured geometry, and no measurement is compared with these runs.
On an arc of radius \numEbArcRadiusM~m around the transmitter, \txtEbArcPanels{} tiles of
$\numEbArcCellsU\times\numEbArcCellsV$ cells act as one surface at \numEbFcGHz~GHz; two receivers
\numEbArcRxDistM~m from the transmitter each have their own exact optimum, and a switch sends
one command per tile for the same time. The cell states have unit amplitude (ASSUMED),
transitions follow the complex-linear path (ENG), and the plate blocking the line of sight
spares the RIS hops although it crosses \numEbArcBlockerCrossed{} of the
\numEbArcBlockerCells{} transmitter-to-cell segments, an idealized regime
(Appendix~\ref{app:cases}). QPSK packets of \numEbPreamble{} preamble and
\numEbPayload{} payload symbols last \numEbPacketMs~ms, the receiver equalizes once from the
preamble, and $E_s/N_0=\numEbEsnoDb$~dB at RX1.

\claim{C-EB-MECH}In the mechanism experiment (E-B1) the surface switches to the
configuration of RX2 during a packet to RX1. VA-5 checks every step switch inside a packet
(\numVaFiveArcTotal{} on the arc, \numVaFiveTotal{} in a lab-like geometry): the earliest
affected sample of RX1 is exactly $\mathrm{to\_ticks}(\tsw+\tau_{\mathrm{RIS}\to\mathrm{RX1}})$,
with the delay of each tile on the arc, and every earlier sample is identical bit for bit to the
run without a switch. With the line of sight blocked (Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(a)), the switch
changes the channel of RX1 by \numEbArcBlockedChangeDb~dB and
\numEbArcBlockedRotDeg$^\circ$. Symbols before it keep their error vector magnitude (EVM) of
about \numEbArcRefEvmDb~dB; after it the equalizer, trained on the old channel, sees an error
as large as the signal, and \numEbArcOneErrors{} of \numEbOneBits{} payload bits fail when the
switch comes a quarter into the packet. A linear transition of \numEbTransLongUs~$\mu$s spreads
the damage over more symbols but still costs \numEbArcLinErrors{} bits. With the line of sight
present, the switch changes the channel by \numEbArcLosChangeDb~dB and
\numEbArcLosRotDeg$^\circ$, the payload EVM rises to \numEbArcLosEvmDb~dB, and the packet loses
\numEbArcLosOneErrors{} of its \numEbOneBits{} payload bits: with a strong line of sight QPSK
tolerates the wrong configuration.

\claim{C-EB-TIMING}In the timing experiment (E-B2) \numEbPackets{} packets alternate between
the two receivers, separated by guards of $T_{\mathrm{g}}$. A controller sends one message per
guard, which arrives after a modelled latency $\Lambda=\Lambda_0+JU$, $\Lambda_0=\numEbLatencyMs$~ms,
$U$ uniform on $[0,1]$. Timed commands carry a switch time at the guard centre, are issued
\numEbLeadsMs~ms ahead and apply on arrival if late; commands without a time apply on arrival
(asap), as untimed UHD commands do~\cite{UHDManual}. The whole grid was fixed before any point
ran (Table~\ref{tab:eb2grid}; Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(b): $T_{\mathrm{g}}=\numEbGuardMs$~ms).
\claim{C-EB-CLOSED}A packet is exposed when its switch lands after the preamble
($T_{\mathrm{pre}}$), with probability $P(\Lambda>T_{\mathrm{g}}+T_{\mathrm{pre}})$ for asap
commands and $P(\Lambda>\ell+T_{\mathrm{g}}/2+T_{\mathrm{pre}})$ for timed ones with lead $\ell$.
With the line of sight blocked, the packet error rate (PER) follows this closed form (asap,
$J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms: \numEbCfAsapOneMeasPct~\% against \numEbCfAsapOnePct~\%; timed,
\numEbLeadMidMs~ms lead, $J=\numEbJitterMaxMs$~ms: \numEbCfTimedThreeMeasPct~\% against
\numEbCfTimedThreePct~\%), exceeding it by at most \numEbCfMaxExcessPts{} points. The timing
rules are thus designed properties of the semantics, which the emulator reproduces on IQ
samples; the asap threshold moves with the guard (at $J=\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms,
\numEbAsapShortGuardPct~\% lost with $T_{\mathrm{g}}=\numEbGuardShortMs$~ms, none with
\numEbGuardLongMs~ms). The waveform adds the cost of exposure: a switch early in the preamble
costs nothing, one late in it causes the excess, and with the line of sight present
\numEbLosLoExposedMinPct--\numEbLosLoExposedMaxPct~\% of the exposed packets fail at
\numEbArcEsnoLowDb~dB and at most \numEbLosHiPerMaxPct~\% of all packets at \numEbEsnoDb~dB.

\claim{C-EB-TICK}A third semantics, a constructed worst case, latches commands on a free-running
\numEbTickMs~ms timer not aligned with the packets. Its closed-form exposure, set by the timer
phase (Appendix~\ref{app:cases}), is \numEbCfTickZeroMinPct--\numEbCfTickZeroMaxPct~\% without
jitter over the guards (Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(b): \numEbArcTickPerMinPct--\numEbArcTickPerMaxPct~\%
lost), so the timer period against the frame decides, not the controller. RIS experiments with
5G NR testbeds also switch on an uncoordinated timer~\cite{Yashvanth2026Random,Kiran2026Random},
but with a period of seconds.

\claim{C-EB-SERVED}These runs are lockstep with a modelled latency. Served in paced mode, the
asap latency reached a p99 of \numBfourVisPninetynineMinMs--\numBfourVisPninetynineMaxMs~ms
and \numBfourLateRepMinPct{} to \numBfourLateRepMaxPct~\% of timed commands with a
\numBfourLeadMs~ms lead were late (Section~\ref{sec:wc-loop}), so the regime
$J\le\numEbJitterOneMs$~ms of Fig.~\ref{fig:F9} is not yet reachable through the served path.
\claim{C-EB-LABLIKE}The lab-like geometry (Fig.~\ref{fig:F9}(c), (d); Appendix~\ref{app:cases})
supports this picture.

\subsection{Signaling delay in closed-loop binary RIS control}\label{sec:cases-ea}
\claim{C-EA-SETUP}A user walks a corridor of \numEaLengthM~m as a one-dimensional random
waypoint at \numEaSpeed~m/s past a single row of on--off cells at \numEaHeightM~m
(\numEaCellsOne{} cells at \numEaFcOneGHz~GHz, \numEaCellsTwo{} at \numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz); a
partition attenuates the line of sight by \numEaLossDb~dB but spares the RIS hops, although it
crosses the path from the access point to every cell, an idealized regime. The exact optimum
and the activation heuristic of~\cite{Mishra2025WCL} (rule in Appendix~\ref{app:cases}) act on
position reports $\tau_{\mathrm{SUI}}$ old; uniform random activation (URAP) and a bandit over
a codebook of exact optima, with one power report per $\tau_{\mathrm{SUI}}$, need no position.
Hypotheses and ranges preceded the sweep; the committed decision rules followed it
(Appendix~\ref{app:cases}). \claim{C-EA-EVAL}The points come not from the
engine's \texttt{run\_batch} but from an offline vectorized evaluator of the same model (channel
on a grid of \numEaFineGridMm~mm or finer, decisions on a \numEaCoarseGridCm~cm grid, each
\numEaStepMs~ms step evaluated at its midpoint, a rule adopted after a short IQ run), over
\numEaBatches{} batches of \numEaPeriods{} mean movement periods of one trajectory.

\claim{C-EA-RESULT}With instantaneous position knowledge the exact optimum gains
\numEaGainOpt~dB over RIS absent at \numEaFcOneGHz~GHz (\numCiPct\,\% interval
\numEaGainOptLo--\numEaGainOptHi~dB, mean of the per-batch dB gains), and \numEaGainOptTwo~dB
at \numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz. We measure the decay with delay (Fig.~\ref{fig:F10}) by the retention
$\rho(\tau)=(P(\tau)-P_{\mathrm{stale}})/(P(0)-P_{\mathrm{stale}})$ in linear power, where
$P_{\mathrm{stale}}$ uses a configuration optimal for an unrelated position. Half the benefit
is lost at $\tau_{1/2}=\numEaTauHalf$~s (\numCiPct\,\% interval
\numEaTauHalfLo--\numEaTauHalfHi~s), and at \numEaFcTwoGHz~GHz at
$\tau_{1/2}=\numEaTauHalfTwo$~s. In units of $\lambda/v$ the two half-lives are
\numEaTauOverLambdaV{} and \numEaTauOverLambdaVTwo: the time scale is $\lambda/v$, as the
classical Doppler decorrelation of a moving terminal predicts~\cite{Clarke1968,Jakes1974}, not
the mobility period, and outdated channel knowledge is known to erode RIS gains under
mobility~\cite{Papazafeiropoulos2023Aging,Zhang2022OutdatedCSI}. Both intervals lie inside the
wide ranges fixed before the sweep, a weak test of H-A1 and H-A2. The constant is not universal:
over the \numEaGeometries{} geometries computed (Table~\ref{tab:eageo}), $\tau_{1/2}$ spans
\numEaTauLambdaVMin--\numEaTauLambdaVMax~$\lambda/v$, rising with the partition loss and
falling with the offset of the cells, for one trajectory at one speed. Without the partition,
or with one acting on both paths (Appendix~\ref{app:cases}), the optimum gains
\numEaGainAzero~dB at \numEaFcOneGHz~GHz, with $\tau_{1/2}=\numEaTauHalfAzero$~s. Without
positions, the
bandit beats URAP
at every delay from \numEaTauBanditMinS~s by \numEaBanditMinDb--\numEaBanditMaxDb~dB (H-A3) but
stays near the stale level (\numEaGainBandit~dB against \numEaGainStale~dB).

\claim{C-VA1}As a consistency check, not a finding, \numVaOneRows{} runs of
\numVaOneDurS~s through the served engine, with an external controller in barrier mode and an
external receiver application (one geometry, exact optimum and URAP, delays of
\numVaOneTauMinS{} and \numVaOneTauMaxS~s, mean power alone), agree with the evaluator within
\numVaOneMaxDiffDb~dB, inside the pre-specified limit of \numVaOneTolDb~dB (VA-1,
Fig.~\ref{fig:F10}(c)). The engine's \texttt{run\_batch} reads up to \numVaOneLeftMaxDb~dB
higher and fails that limit (Section~\ref{sec:design-time}); with its start-of-step rule the
evaluator matches it within \numVaOneVecLeftDb~dB, and with all cells on its channel
interpolation errs by at most \numEaInterpMaxDb~dB (Appendix~\ref{app:cases}).

\subsection{Non-ideality ablation}\label{sec:cases-ec}
\claim{C-EC}Because the optimum over a finite alphabet is exact
(Section~\ref{sec:model-nonideal}), the cost of a non-ideality is a short analytic result
rather than the outcome of a heuristic; Appendix~\ref{app:cases} gives the loss of the
optimized RIS term relative to ideal continuous phase per alphabet and geometry. The results
agree with prior work~\cite{WuZhang2020Discrete,Abeywickrama2020Practical}. One-bit control
costs \numEcOneBitMin--\numEcOneBitMax~dB, less than the large-$N$ closed-form loss of
\numEcOneBitEta~dB, because a finite panel in a fixed geometry is not a random phase ensemble.
The laboratory states, with magnitudes ASSUMED to be unit, are nearly antipodal (in
$\Gamma=m\pm d$, $|d|$ is \numEcAbsD~dB and $|m|$ is \numEcAbsM~dB) and behave like ideal one-bit
states (within \numEcLabVsOneBitMaxDb~dB). Unequal amplitudes cost up to \numEcHalfMaxDb~dB when
one state reflects at half amplitude, an efficiency of one half adds about \numEcEffExtraDb~dB,
and designing for an ideal alphabet but deploying on the true states costs
\numEcDesignPenaltyMinDb--\numEcDesignPenaltyMaxDb~dB more than designing for them.

\subsection{Integration with external IQ applications}\label{sec:cases-ee}

\claim{C-EE}Besides V-CFO-3 and VA-1, which ran with external processes, the raw
request--reply mode (Section~\ref{sec:design-if}) ran with unmodified GNU Radio
\txtEeGrVersion{} (Ubuntu package) as the peer, in a separate process that knows nothing of the
emulator: its stock ZMQ request and reply blocks and a DQPSK modem of stock blocks with blind
timing and carrier recovery. In the scene (SIM), transmitter and receiver stand \numEeDistM~m
from an ideal 1-bit RIS of \numEeCells{} cells at \numEeFcGHz~GHz, with a partition of
\numEeWallDb~dB on the direct path, at \numEeRateMsps~MS/s (Appendix~\ref{app:cases}). \claim{C-EE-LINK}In
\numEeConfigs{} fresh runs of \numEeStaticS~s, one per RIS configuration, GNU Radio makes no
symbol error with focusing, its error rates do not decrease as the configuration's gain
falls, and the random configuration loses every block; the gains lie within
\numEeAfMaxDiffDb~dB of an array-factor prediction, an internal consistency check, not a
measured RIS gain.

\claim{C-EE-TIMING}A further run switched the RIS three times, \numEeSegmentS~s apart, by
commands scheduled over the control socket before virtual time started: each switch was
applied with zero lateness, and the change point of the IQ that GNU Radio received lies at the
logged switch sample (largest offset \numEeCpOffsetMax{} samples). GNU Radio received every
sample, bit for bit the stream that the emulator recorded, and across \txtEeReps{} independent
repetitions of all \numEeRuns{} runs the SHA-256 digests of the received IQ and of GNU Radio's
decisions are identical. \claim{C-EE-SPEED}The runs are lockstep, so the sample exchange paces
the emulator and the wall-clock speed changes how long a run takes, not its samples.
\claim{C-EE-NOTRUN}srsRAN and OpenAirInterface were analysed but not run
(Appendix~\ref{app:cases}).
