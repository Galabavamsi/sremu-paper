\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{R}{econfigurable} intelligent surfaces (RIS) are planar arrays of passive
elements whose reflection states are set by a controller, so that part of the propagation
environment becomes a configurable system component~\cite{DiRenzo2020SRE,Basar2019Access,WuZhang2020IRS}.
Standards bodies now describe their use cases, architectures, channel models and test
methods~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS001_v121,ETSI_GR_RIS002_v121,ETSI_GR_RIS003_v121,ETSI_GS_RIS008}.
Much of the literature asks how a controller should choose a configuration: with discrete
phase states~\cite{WuZhang2020Discrete,Zhang2022OptimalBinary,Zhao2024OptimalDiscrete},
phase-dependent amplitudes~\cite{Abeywickrama2020Practical}, receiver
mobility~\cite{Matthiesen2021Mobility,Mishra2025WCL} or sensing~\cite{Mishra2025NCC}.

Most of these controllers are evaluated with ideal element states, instantaneous
reconfiguration and one channel coefficient per block. Hardware departs from each: unit cells
have state- and angle-dependent responses~\cite{Tang2021PathLoss,Tang2022mmWave} with coupled
amplitude and phase~\cite{Abeywickrama2020Practical}; the specification GS RIS 008 of the
European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) lists reconfiguration time and
modulated-signal quality among the parameters of a RIS test~\cite{ETSI_GS_RIS008}; and a command travels over a control link with its own delay, so a position-driven controller
acts on a stale position~\cite{Mishra2025WCL}. A reconfiguration inside a packet shows in the
received samples, not in a per-block coefficient.

Existing tools cover parts of this problem (Section~\ref{sec:related},
Table~\ref{tab:toolsmain}): coefficient-level simulators run
offline~\cite{Basar2020SimRIS,Burtakov2023QRIS}, Open Radio Access Network (O-RAN) frameworks
loop over coefficients~\cite{ElGhalbzouri2025GoSimRIS,Kayraklik2026ORIX}, and IQ-level
emulators have no RIS model~\cite{Gurses2026ACHEM,OAIrfsim,srsRANZMQ} or fix the RIS channels
in advance~\cite{Tsampazi2025RISColosseum,Tsampazi2025ORISing}.
\claim{C-GAP}To our knowledge, as of 10 Oct 2026, no surveyed tool combines
(i)~multi-node emulation at the level of complex-baseband (IQ) samples behind an interface in the style
of a software-defined radio (SDR); (ii)~RIS configurations issued at run time by an external controller and
applied at an explicit, logged sample; (iii)~per-state responses imported from
measurements as functions of state, frequency, and incidence and reflection angle in one plane; and
(iv)~characterized wall-clock behaviour. Each property exists in some tool; the
contribution of this paper is their integration, together with the evidence needed to
trust the result.

We present \sremu{}, a software-only emulator of a RIS-assisted radio environment at the
level of complex-baseband samples, to which external transmitter, receiver and controller
processes connect through an SDR-style interface. Time is an exact rational sample count.
\claim{C-TIMED-CMD}A RIS command requested for the switch time $\tsw$ at the surface
takes effect on receiver~$r$ at the sample nearest to $(\tsw+\tau_r)f_s$, where $\tau_r$ is
the propagation delay from the surface to that receiver and $f_s$ its sample rate, and
both the requested and the applied sample are logged. The paper makes four contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Timed RIS control in an IQ-level emulator} (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
  \claim{C-CONTRIB-1}The switch time is defined at the surface and applied per receiver, a
  late command keeps one physical switch time for all receivers, and every command is logged;
  in lockstep mode, virtual-time barriers make controller-in-the-loop runs deterministic, in
  process and over the control socket, and a paced mode measures its own timing. The
  interfaces are a Python application programming
  interface (API) shaped after the USRP Hardware Driver (UHD)~\cite{UHDManual}, a framed ZeroMQ
  (ZMQ) data plane, and a response-table schema aligned with the parameters of ETSI GS RIS
  008~\cite{ETSI_GS_RIS008}.
\item \emph{A verified physical model with stated validity limits}
  (Sections~\ref{sec:model},~\ref{sec:vv}). \claim{C-CONTRIB-2}An area-tied aperture kernel
  with a cell model and a model card; an import path for per-state tables, tested but not
  used here with a measured table; \numOracleCount{} analytic oracle checks and MATLAB
  code-to-code checks; agreement of the model class with third-party chamber measurements,
  inherited from a published path-loss model that the emulator reproduces~\cite{Tang2022mmWave};
  a consistency check against a laboratory measurement from the author's earlier group work under a pre-specified
  plan hash-locked before unblinding, with its null result and measurement-design lesson; and
  carrier-frequency-offset (CFO) and bit-error-rate checks against the Cram\'er--Rao bound and
  theory.
\item \emph{Characterized wall-clock behaviour} (Section~\ref{sec:wallclock}).
  \claim{C-CONTRIB-3}Definitions of lockstep, paced, headless and served operation, the
  real-time factor (RTF) and deadline misses; scaling over links, elements, surfaces, threads,
  sample rates and feature sets, including the native ZMQ data plane, on AWS Linux servers;
  and, for a controller served over the control socket, command latency and timed-command
  exactness, though page deadlines fail our Sustainable rule.
\item \emph{Case studies of timed control} (Section~\ref{sec:cases}).
  \claim{C-CONTRIB-4}At the sample level, in lockstep with an in-process controller and a
  modelled command latency: what a reconfiguration inside a packet does to the symbols, and
  when timestamped commands, commands applied on arrival and switching on a free-running
  timer lose packets. On channel coefficients (for E-A an offline evaluator that agrees with
  the sample-level VA-1 runs within the pre-specified tolerance): the time scale on
  which signaling delay erodes position-driven binary control in a blocked corridor, and a
  non-ideality ablation.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Scope.} \claim{C-SCOPE}\sremu{} is not a UHD drop-in: it offers a UHD-shaped API, and
virtual-USRP emulation is a separate line of work~\cite{Gurses2026ACHEM}. It is not a
hard-real-time emulator, not a ray tracer, and not a model of mutual coupling beyond what
per-state tables measured with identical neighbours contain~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS003_v121}.
Fields are scalar and co-polar, RIS paths are single-bounce, and wall reflections are of
order one. Paced-mode timing is measured and reported per platform, never assumed.
Appendices~\ref{app:survey}--\ref{app:cases}, after the references, extend
Sections~\ref{sec:related}--\ref{sec:cases}; Appendices~\ref{app:oracles}--\ref{app:provenance}
describe the oracles, the build and the provenance of every result.
