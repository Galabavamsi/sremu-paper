\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{R}{econfigurable} intelligent surfaces (RIS) are planar arrays of passive
elements whose reflection states are set by a controller, so that part of the propagation
environment becomes a configurable system component~\cite{DiRenzo2020SRE,Basar2019Access,WuZhang2020IRS}.
Standards bodies now describe their use cases, architectures, channel models and test
methods~\cite{ETSI_GR_RIS001_v121,ETSI_GR_RIS002_v121,ETSI_GR_RIS003_v121,ETSI_GS_RIS008}.
A large part of the literature asks how a controller should choose a configuration: with
discrete phase states~\cite{WuZhang2020Discrete,Zhang2022OptimalBinary,Zhao2024OptimalDiscrete},
with phase-dependent element amplitudes~\cite{Abeywickrama2020Practical}, under receiver
mobility~\cite{Matthiesen2021Mobility,Mishra2025WCL}, or for sensing~\cite{Mishra2025NCC}.

Most of these controllers are evaluated under three simplifications. Element states are
ideal (unit amplitude with any phase, or a uniform discrete alphabet), reconfiguration is
instantaneous, and the channel is one coefficient per block rather than a waveform.
Hardware departs from each of them. Measured unit cells have state-dependent and
angle-dependent responses~\cite{Tang2021PathLoss,Tang2022mmWave}, amplitude and phase are
coupled~\cite{Abeywickrama2020Practical}, and the specification GS RIS 008 of the
European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) lists reconfiguration time
and modulated-signal quality among the parameters a RIS test has to
measure~\cite{ETSI_GS_RIS008}. A configuration command also travels over a control link
with its own delay, so a position-driven controller acts on a stale
position~\cite{Mishra2025WCL}. Some consequences of these effects, such as a
reconfiguration that lands inside a packet, appear in the received samples and are
invisible to a per-block coefficient model.

Existing tools cover parts of this problem (Section~\ref{sec:related} and
Table~\ref{tab:tools}). Coefficient-level simulators model RIS channels
offline~\cite{Basar2020SimRIS,Burtakov2023QRIS}. Open Radio Access Network (O-RAN)
frameworks close a controller loop
around such coefficients~\cite{ElGhalbzouri2025GoSimRIS,Kayraklik2026ORIX}. IQ-level
emulators exchange complex-baseband samples with radio software but have no RIS
model~\cite{Gurses2026ACHEM,OAIrfsim,srsRANZMQ}, or install RIS channels that are fixed
before the experiment starts~\cite{Tsampazi2025RISColosseum,Tsampazi2025ORISing}.
\claim{C-GAP}To our knowledge, as of 10 Oct 2026, no surveyed open tool combines
(i)~multi-node emulation at the level of complex-baseband (IQ) samples behind an interface in the style
of a software-defined radio (SDR); (ii)~RIS configurations issued at run time by an external controller and
applied at an explicit, logged sample; (iii)~per-state responses imported from
measurements as functions of state, frequency, incidence angle and reflection angle; and
(iv)~characterized wall-clock behaviour. Each property exists in some tool. The
contribution of this paper is their integration, together with the evidence needed to
trust the result.

We present \sremu{}, a software-only emulator of a RIS-assisted radio environment at the
level of complex-baseband samples. External transmitter, receiver and controller
processes connect to it through an SDR-style interface. Time is an exact rational sample
count. \claim{C-TIMED-CMD}A RIS command requested for the switch time $\tsw$ at the surface
takes effect on receiver~$r$ at the sample nearest to $(\tsw+\tau_r)f_s$, where $\tau_r$ is
the propagation delay from the surface to that receiver and $f_s$ its sample rate, and
both the requested and the applied sample are logged. Element responses are tables
$\Gamma(s,f,\theta_i,\theta_s)$ over state, frequency and the two angles, with provenance
and load-time checks, and the propagation kernel ties each cell's contribution to its
area. The paper makes four contributions.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Timed RIS control in an IQ-level emulator} (Section~\ref{sec:design}).
  \claim{C-CONTRIB-1}The switch time is defined at the surface and applied per receiver;
  a late command keeps one physical switch time for all receivers; every command is
  logged. A lockstep mode with virtual-time barriers makes controller-in-the-loop runs
  deterministic, and a wall-clock-paced mode measures and reports its own timing. The
  interfaces are a Python application programming interface (API) shaped after the USRP
  Hardware Driver (UHD)~\cite{UHDManual}, a framed ZeroMQ (ZMQ) data plane, and a response-table
  schema whose fields are aligned with the parameters of ETSI GS RIS
  008~\cite{ETSI_GS_RIS008}.
\item \emph{A verified physical model with a stated validity domain}
  (Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:vv}). \claim{C-CONTRIB-2}An area-tied
  physical-optics kernel with an explicit cell model and a model card
  (Table~\ref{tab:modelcard}); \numOracleCount{} analytic oracle checks taken from the
  continuous-integration record; MATLAB code-to-code comparisons; agreement with
  published path-loss model curves~\cite{Tang2021PathLoss,Tang2022mmWave}; a pre-registered
  consistency check against digitized laboratory data; and the root cause of a
  carrier-frequency-offset (CFO) gain error in the legacy code, whose estimate slope moves
  from \numCfoSlopeLegacy{} to \numCfoSlopeFixed{}.
\item \emph{Characterized wall-clock behaviour} (Section~\ref{sec:wallclock}).
  \claim{C-CONTRIB-3}Explicit definitions of lockstep and paced operation; the real-time
  factor (RTF), deadline misses and latency percentiles; scaling over links, elements,
  surfaces, sample rates and feature sets on a laptop and a Linux server; and a fitted
  cost model.
\item \emph{Results that need a sample-level emulator} (Section~\ref{sec:cases}).
  \claim{C-CONTRIB-4}The timing of a reconfiguration inside a frame and the value of
  timestamped commands; the time scale on which signaling delay erodes position-driven
  binary control in a blocked corridor; a non-ideality ablation; and a model-based
  dimensioning of an FM-band surface.
\end{itemize}
