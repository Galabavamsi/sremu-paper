\claim{C-WC-LOCKSTEP}Of \numBtwoPlanned{} planned B2 points, \numBtwoRun{} ran; the other
\numBtwoPruned{} were not started because a smaller point of their series had fallen below the
pilot floor or failed. Of the points that ran, \numBtwoOk{} completed, \numBtwoBelowFloor{} of
the heaviest mobility points fell below the pilot floor (a pilot estimate, no measurement),
and \numBtwoFailed{} failed. Every failure was a point with the native ZMQ data plane (F5):
three receive time-outs came from an error of the benchmark, whose served run ended before
the clients' streams, and the fourth, also a benchmark error, came from summarizing a point
whose pilot was below the floor. Both were fixed in the benchmark, not in the emulator, and F5 was rerun
(B2-F45).

\claim{C-WC-ISOLATION}Concurrent points share the last-level cache, memory bandwidth and the
power budget of the host. Rerun alone, the \numBtwoIsoPoints{} reference points of the
isolation check were up to \numBtwoIsoMaxPct~\% faster (ratio \numBtwoIsoMin--\numBtwoIsoMax),
so the packed values in Fig.~\ref{fig:F7} are conservative. The link limit lies within this
margin: at \numBtwoLsixteen{} links the packed RTF is \numBtwoLsixteenMin--\numBtwoLsixteenMax{}
over $N$, while the same base configuration measured alone in B1 reached \numBoneLsixteenRtf.
We therefore state the limit as \numBtwoMaxLinks{} links packed and between \numBtwoMaxLinks{}
and \numBtwoLsixteen{} links alone.

\subsection{Data plane}
\claim{C-WC-DATAPLANE}The F5 figure is end to end: the virtual time delivered to the receiving
client process divided by the wall time from the served engine's warm-up boundary to the
client's last sample, so a client cannot shorten it. The \numBtwoFfiveStressRuns{} stress runs
of the base point reached RTF \numBtwoFfiveStressMin--\numBtwoFfiveStressMax{} (median
\numBtwoFfiveStressMed) with no stall and no bad packet. In \numBtwoFfivePauseRuns{} runs whose
receiving clients paused \numBtwoFfivePauseS~s before their warm-up completed, it fell to
\numBtwoFfivePauseRtf{} (median), while the client's own read rate, a figure of the client
alone that such a pause inflates, reached \numBtwoFfivePauseClientRtf. The single isolation reference point of
B2-F45 ran \numBtwoFfiveIsoRatio{} times faster alone. In the E-F measurement (lockstep, on a
busy \txtHostServer{}), through the UHD-shaped API a buffer of \numApiBufferSamples{} samples
took \numApiSendPfiftyUs~$\mu$s to send in process and \numZmqRttPfiftyUs~$\mu$s to send and
receive across two processes (medians; p99 \numApiSendPninetynineUs{} and
\numZmqRttPninetynineUs~$\mu$s), and streaming over native ZMQ sustained
\numApiThroughputMsps~MS/s; receiving a buffer in
process, which includes computing it, took \numApiRecvPfiftyUs~$\mu$s (median), alternating
send and receive sustained \numApiInprocMsps~MS/s, and the ZMQ rate was measured with buffers of
\numApiZmqBufferSamples{} samples. Other work of the regeneration job kept the host up to
\numEfLatencyBusyMaxPct~\% busy during these measurements.

\subsection{Paced series and short pages}
\claim{C-WC-PACED}The \numBthreeOneLinks{} links with \numBthreeOnePageUs~$\mu$s pages missed
at most a fraction \numBthreeOneMiss{} of the deadlines at \numSustainSlackMs~ms slack in a
repetition. The single link at \numBthreeTopMsps~MS/s uses pages of
\numBthreeTopPageUs~$\mu$s (p99 \numBthreeTopPninetynineUs~$\mu$s, miss rate
\numBthreeTopMiss); at \numBthreeMidMsps~MS/s the pages last \numBthreeMidPageUs~$\mu$s and at
\numBthreeLowMsps~MS/s \numBthreeLowPageMs~ms. With pages of \numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s the
\numBthreeHalfLinks{} links have a worst p99 of \numBthreeHalfPninetynineUs~$\mu$s; four
surfaces sustain as many links (\numBthreeFourRisLinks), and $N=\numBthreeBigN$ one.

\claim{C-WC-SHORTPAGES}Two mechanisms end the short-page series. At \numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s,
\numBthreeHalfFailLinks{} links keep the engine busy for a median \numBthreeHalfFailUtil{} of
each page and miss \numBthreeHalfFailMissPct~\% of the deadlines: computation. The
\numBthreeQuarterPageUs~$\mu$s series and the $N=\numBthreeBigN$ series instead passed two
repetitions of their last point and failed the third, through stalls of up to
\numBthreeQuarterStallMs~ms (miss rate \numBthreeQuarterFailMiss) and
\numBthreeBigNStallMs~ms; the runs record no stage breakdown that would identify the stalls.
With mobility (F3), one link misses \numBthreeFthreeMissPct~\% of the deadlines at
\numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s pages in the single repetition run: the geometry snapshot is
recomputed every \numBthreeFthreeTuMs~ms, \numBthreeFthreeSnapPct~\% of the pages hold a
recomputation, and such a page takes up to \numBthreeFthreeUtilMs~ms (p99), although the mean
load stays below the wall clock. Not tried: mobility at other page durations or link counts,
more than one surface with mobility, and paced runs with client processes on the data plane.

\subsection{Control loop}
\claim{C-WC-LOCKCMD}At the highest lockstep rate the last command of a run switches after the
end of the run and is never applied; the check counts such commands as scheduled after the end,
not as failures. The per-command engine cost of Section~\ref{sec:wc-loop} (barrier, decision and
configuration change) holds at \numBfourRateMidHz~Hz and above, and is computed from the RTF
with and without the loop. It includes splitting the page at the switch and rebuilding the
link statics; the laptop run B6 times a configuration change with the geometry cached at
\numBsixCombMinUs--\numBsixCombMaxUs~$\mu$s, and these runs record no stage breakdown that
would apportion the rest.

\claim{C-WC-PACEDCMD}Served paced engines render small pages serially, let idle OpenMP workers
sleep and exempt their objects from garbage collection during the loop. A served repetition is
\emph{backlogged} when its median page latency exceeds one page or more than
\numBacklogMissPct~\% of its pages miss a slack of \numBacklogSlackMs~ms; a backlogged
repetition's command latencies measure the backlog, so its late and exact fractions are not
counted. In the served-1ms plan, timed commands carried leads of
\numBfourLeadMinMs--\numBfourLeadMaxMs~ms. A timed command is late when the engine takes it
after its switch sample was computed. At its receipt the switch lies a margin ahead of the
pacer's schedule: the lead minus the delay to the server and the error of the client's
estimate of virtual time. The p1 of this margin per repetition was
\numBfourMarginTwoPoneMs--\numBfourMarginTwoPoneMaxMs~ms with a \numBfourLeadMinMs~ms lead,
where the late fraction of every repetition exceeded the share of commands below its p1, and
\numBfourMarginFourPoneMs--\numBfourMarginFourPoneMaxMs~ms with \numBfourLeadOkMs~ms, where it
fell short of that share in every repetition. A single margin threshold would thus lie between
about
\numBfourMarginTwoPoneMaxMs{} and \numBfourMarginFourPoneMs~ms, of the order of one page plus its
computation; the runs do not record when the engine took each command, so this is an
inference. Pooled over the repetitions, late fractions were at most
\numBfourLateFourMaxPct~\% (\numBfourLeadOkMs~ms; \numBfourLateFourRepMaxPct~\% in one
repetition) and \numBfourLateSixMaxPct~\% (\numBfourLeadSixMs--\numBfourLeadMaxMs~ms).
The client's round trip of a command reached a p99 of up to \numBfourRttPninetynineMaxMs~ms;
the apply delay (Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(d)), which excludes the lead, had medians up to
\numBfourApplyPfiftyMaxMs~ms and a p99 up to \numBfourApplyPninetynineMaxMs~ms; its largest
value, \numBfourApplyMaxMs~ms, came with a page stall of about that length in one repetition,
and asap commands had rare visible-latency outliers of up to \numBfourVisMaxMs~ms. The latency floor
is consistent with the engine's work on the page with the configuration change; that the spread comes
from the hand-over between the control-socket thread and the paced loop, which share one
Python interpreter, is a hypothesis. Page deadlines are missed between commands too: at
\numBfourRateLowHz~commands/s the miss rate at \numBacklogSlackMs~ms slack was at most
\numBfourMissFiveLowMax. At \numBfourRateHighHz~commands/s page latencies grew to
\numBfourPageLatMaxS~s.

The original plan (B4-classic, another instance) gives the same picture with pages of
\numBfourClassicPageUs~$\mu$s and a lead of \numBfourLeadMinMs~ms:
\numBfourClassicMissMinPct--\numBfourClassicMissMaxPct~\% of the pages missed the
\numSustainSlackMs~ms deadline at \numBfourRateLowHz{} and \numBfourRateMidHz~commands/s,
against at most \numBthreeClassicRefMiss{} for the same links and pages headless (B3), so the
served baseline is not marginal headless; \numBfourClassicLateMinPct--\numBfourClassicLateMaxPct~\%
of the timed commands were late, the asap visible-latency p99 stayed at or below
\numBfourClassicVisPninetynineMaxMs~ms, and from \numBfourRateHighHz~commands/s on every
repetition was backlogged, with \numBfourClassicAchievedMin--\numBfourClassicAchievedMax{}
commands/s achieved.

\subsection{Cost model}
\claim{C-WC-COSTMODEL}B1 fits a page cost model, a constant plus linear terms
for snapshots, fractional-delay filtering, receivers and paths, to \numBonePoints{} engine
points measured on an otherwise idle server. The fit is indicative: its relative RMS error
is \numBoneRelRmsPct~\% and its largest error \numBoneRelMaxPct~\%. The constant term,
\numBoneFixedUs~$\mu$s per page, is \numBoneFixedSharePct~\% of the single-link page cost of
\numBoneBasePageUs~$\mu$s, so short pages pay mostly fixed cost. The largest errors are
underestimates of mobility points. There, a snapshot costs about \numBoneSnapMs~ms plus
\numBoneSnapLinkMs~ms per link, with little dependence on the number of surface rows and
quadrature points $RQ$ at the sizes of these points; a diagnostic fit with these terms reaches
\numBoneExtRelRmsPct~\%, but with \numBoneExtCoeffs{} coefficients for \numBonePoints{} points it
is not used for prediction, and at the larger element counts of B2 the snapshot cost does grow
with $N$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}(a)).
The kernel itself scales as expected: the snapshot time grows as
$(RQ)^{\numBoneSnapExpNumpy}$ on the NumPy reference backend for $RQ$ from \numBoneRqMin{} to
\numBoneRqMax, and as $(RQ)^{\numBoneSnapExpNumba}$ with numba up to \numBoneRqMaxNumba, where
fixed costs dominate the small sizes.

\subsection{Cross-tool context}
\claim{C-WC-CROSSTOOL}For context, B6 times one evaluation of the same near-field surface sum
on the laptop for $N=\numBsixNmin$ to \numBsixNmax{} elements with \sremu{} and with MATLAB
\txtBsixMatlab{}; where both compute the same model the results agree to
\numBsixAgreeDb~dB. \sremu{} takes \numBsixFullMinUs--\numBsixFullMaxUs~$\mu$s for new
positions and \numBsixCombMinUs--\numBsixCombMaxUs~$\mu$s for a configuration change with the
geometry cached; a vectorized MATLAB script takes \numBsixVecMinUs--\numBsixVecMaxUs~$\mu$s, a
per-element MATLAB loop \numBsixLoopMinUs--\numBsixLoopMaxUs~$\mu$s, and the toolbox's
far-field \texttt{phased.RISReflector}, a different model,
\numBsixRisrMinUs--\numBsixRisrMaxUs~$\mu$s. These numbers are not a ranking: the calls do
different work (the \sremu{} times include Python dispatch and the line-of-sight path; the
MATLAB times exclude its start-up), they come from one machine whose other processes were busy
for \numBsixBusyPct~\% of the time during the \sremu{} timings, and a streaming emulator's cost
is per page, not per call.
