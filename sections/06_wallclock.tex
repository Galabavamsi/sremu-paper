\section{Wall-Clock Behaviour and Scalability}\label{sec:wallclock}

\claim{C-WC-DEFS}Lockstep runs advance virtual time as fast as the inputs and the host allow
and never drop a sample; paced runs release page $j$ of a rate group at the wall time $R_j$ of
Section~\ref{sec:design-time}. The metrics are defined as follows. The real-time
factor (RTF) is the virtual time advanced, taken as the minimum over the received streams of
samples produced divided by the sample rate, per unit of wall time; a run with RTF${}\geq1$
keeps up with the wall clock. Aggregate throughput is the number of received samples, summed
over streams, per second of wall time. The latency of a paced page is the time from $R_j$ to
its delivery, and the page misses its deadline at slack $s$ when this latency exceeds $s$, so
the miss rate at slack $s$ is the complementary distribution of the page latency at $s$.
Because a page is computed from its release on, every page misses a deadline of zero slack. A
configuration is \emph{Sustainable} when, over at least \numSustainMinDurationS~s after a
warm-up of \numSustainWarmupS~s, fewer than a fraction \numSustainMissRate{} of its pages miss
the deadline at \numSustainSlackMs~ms slack, no input sample arrives late and no output page
overflows. The visible latency of a command is the wall time from its receipt at the emulator
to the delivery of the page that holds its switch sample; its lateness is the distance in
samples between the requested and the applied switch sample.

\claim{C-WC-HOSTS}Table~\ref{tab:platforms} lists the hosts. The lockstep benchmarks (B1,
B2) ran on an AWS \txtHostServer{} instance and the paced and control-loop benchmarks (B3,
B4) on two AWS \txtHostPaced{} instances, with the engine pinned to \numPacedEngineCpus{}
logical CPUs and the rest of the host idle apart from the B4 command client; the cross-tool
context (B6) comes from a laptop. All AWS runs used one emulator commit. B2 packed up to \numBtwoSlots{} points onto the server at a time, each
pinned to \numBtwoSlotCpus{} logical CPUs of whole cores in one NUMA node. A B3 point runs up
to \numBthreeReps{} repetitions of \numBthreeMeasureS~s, each with at least
\numBthreeMinPages{} pages, and counts as Sustainable when every repetition is; an
unsustainable repetition ends the point. The paced runs of B3 are headless: the transmit
streams are generated in the emulator process and the received pages stay there, so client
and transport costs are excluded. The benchmark scenes have one
carrier, line-of-sight and single-bounce surface paths, and a non-zero surface path on every
link; they load the engine and carry no physical result. A link is a transmitter--receiver
pair, so a scene with $L$ links has $n_{\mathrm{tx}}n_{\mathrm{rx}}=L$; for example,
\numBtwoMaxLinks{} links are \numBtwoLimTx{} transmitters and \numBtwoLimRx{} receivers.

\subsection{Lockstep scaling}
\claim{C-WC-LOCKSTEP}Of \numBtwoPlanned{} planned B2 points, \numBtwoRun{} ran:
\numBtwoOk{} completed, \numBtwoBelowFloor{} of the heaviest mobility points fell below the
pilot floor and were not measured, and \numBtwoFailed{} failed. Every failure was a point
with the native ZMQ data plane (feature set F5): three receive time-outs caused by a transport
stall that is being corrected, and one benchmark error on a point below the floor. F5 is
therefore left out of Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}, and its RTF at the base point (\numBtwoFfiveRtf)
awaits the rerun. One
link at \numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s through one surface of \numBtwoBaseN{} elements, with a static
geometry and thermal noise (F1), runs at RTF \numBtwoBaseRtf. In static scenes the element
count barely matters: from $N=\numBtwoNmin$ to \numBtwoNmax{} the single-link RTF stays within
\numBtwoNspreadPct~\%, because the geometry snapshot is computed once
(Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}(a)). With mobility (F3) the snapshot is recomputed on the automatic
snapshot grid and $N$ matters: the single-link RTF falls from \numBtwoFthreeRtfSmall{} at
$N=\numBtwoNmin$ to \numBtwoFthreeRtfLarge{} at $N=\numBtwoNmax$. A static scene keeps
RTF${}\geq1$ up to \numBtwoMaxLinks{} links at \numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s for every $N$, and a single
link keeps up at \numBtwoTopMsps~MS/s (RTF \numBtwoTopRtf). At one link, the cumulative feature
sets of Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}(b) reach RTF \numBtwoRtfFzero{} without noise (F0),
\numBtwoBaseRtf{} with noise, \numBtwoRtfFtwo{} with oscillator impairments (F2),
\numBtwoRtfFthree{} with mobility (F3) and \numBtwoRtfFfour{} with recording (F4). With noise
(F1), aggregate throughput peaks at \numBtwoAggMaxMsps~MS/s, with \numBtwoAggMaxRx{} receivers at
\numBtwoAggMaxRate~MS/s and an RTF below one (Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}(c)).

\claim{C-WC-ISOLATION}Concurrent points share the last-level cache, memory bandwidth and the
power budget of the host. Rerun alone, the \numBtwoIsoPoints{} reference points of the
isolation check were up to \numBtwoIsoMaxPct~\% faster (ratio \numBtwoIsoMin--\numBtwoIsoMax),
so the packed values in Fig.~\ref{fig:F7} are conservative. The link limit lies within this
margin: at \numBtwoLsixteen{} links the packed RTF is \numBtwoLsixteenMin--\numBtwoLsixteenMax{}
over $N$, while the same base configuration measured alone in B1 reached \numBoneLsixteenRtf.
We therefore state the limit as \numBtwoMaxLinks{} links packed and between \numBtwoMaxLinks{}
and \numBtwoLsixteen{} links alone.

\subsection{Paced operation}
\claim{C-WC-PACED}Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(b) gives the largest Sustainable configuration of each B3
series. At \numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s with one surface of \numBtwoBaseN{} elements and noise, the
emulator sustains \numBthreeOneLinks{} links with pages of \numBthreeOnePageUs~$\mu$s, with a
worst p99 page latency of \numBthreeOnePninetynineUs~$\mu$s and a worst miss rate of
\numBthreeOneMiss{} at \numSustainSlackMs~ms slack. With pages of \numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s it
sustains one link (p99 \numBthreeHalfPninetynineUs~$\mu$s), and with pages of
\numBthreeQuarterPageUs~$\mu$s \numBthreeQuarterLinks{} links. A single link at
\numBthreeTopMsps~MS/s (pages of \numBthreeTopPageUs~$\mu$s) is Sustainable with p99
\numBthreeTopPninetynineUs~$\mu$s and miss rate \numBthreeTopMiss; at
\numBthreeMidMsps~MS/s (pages of \numBthreeMidPageUs~$\mu$s) the limit is \numBthreeMidLinks{}
links. With pages of \numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s, four surfaces sustain
\numBthreeFourRisLinks{} links, more than one surface does, and $N=\numBthreeBigN$ one link:
at such pages the limit is not set by the computation alone.

\claim{C-WC-SHORTPAGES}At short pages the limit is set by rare stalls, not by the mean load.
Two links with \numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s pages keep the engine busy for a median fraction
\numBthreeTwoLinkUtil{} of each page, yet one of the three repetitions missed
\numBthreeTwoLinkMissMax{} of the deadlines (the other two at most
\numBthreeTwoLinkMissOther): a few stalls of up to \numBthreeStallMaxMs~ms each delay dozens of
short pages. With mobility (F3), no configuration is Sustainable at
\numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s pages; one link misses \numBthreeFthreeMissPct~\% of the deadlines.
The geometry snapshot is recomputed every \numBthreeFthreeTuMs~ms, and the page that holds the
recomputation takes up to \numBthreeFthreeUtilMs~ms (p99) although the mean load stays below
the wall clock. The fraction of pages that hold a snapshot, \numBthreeFthreeSnapPct~\%, is close
to the miss rate: the snapshot work is not spread over pages. At \numBthreeLowMsps~MS/s a page
holds \numBthreeLowPage{} samples (\numBthreeLowPageMs~ms) and the engine works for
\numBthreeLowUtilPct~\% of it (median), yet \numBthreeLowMissPages{} pages had latencies of
\numSustainSlackMs~ms to \numBthreeLowMaxMs~ms. The resulting miss rate,
\numBthreeLowMiss{}, misses the threshold in the single repetition run. These delays are not
explained by per-page cost or by timer resolution, since the engine spins rather than
sleeps before releases closer than \numPacedSpinMs~ms; the runs record no stage breakdown that
would identify them.

\subsection{Control loop}
\claim{C-WC-LOCKCMD}In lockstep, controllers issued \numBfourLockCommands{} commands at rates
from \numBfourRateMinHz~Hz to \numBfourRateMaxKHz~kHz, in process and from a separate process
over the control socket, and every lateness value is zero, as the barrier semantics require. At
the highest rate the last command of a run switches after the end of the run and is never
applied; the benchmark's original check counted it as a failure, and the corrected check,
recomputed offline from the same records, passes. The loop costs wall time instead: at
\numBfourRateMidHz~Hz and above, each in-process command (barrier, decision and configuration
change) costs \numBfourCmdCostMinMs--\numBfourCmdCostMaxMs~ms of engine time, from the RTF with
and without the loop, so at \numBfourRateMaxKHz~kHz the RTF drops from \numBfourRtfNone{} to
\numBfourRtfInproc{} in process and to \numBfourRtfRemote{} over the socket.

\claim{C-WC-PACEDCMD}In paced mode with asynchronous commands from an external process, served
over the control socket (B4, \numBfourLinks{} links, pages of \numBfourPageMs~ms), no repetition
was Sustainable (Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(d)). At \numBfourRateLowHz~commands/s,
\numBfourMissLowMinPct--\numBfourMissLowMaxPct~\% of the pages missed the
\numSustainSlackMs~ms deadline, and at \numBfourRateMidHz~commands/s
\numBfourMissMidMinPct--\numBfourMissMidMaxPct~\%; the same two links and pages without the
control socket (B3, on another instance of the same type) missed at most
\numBthreeTwoLinkMissMax{}, the marginal point discussed above. A configuration change costs
the engine about as much time as the slack (see the lockstep cost above), so the page that
holds it, and
often the next, miss their deadlines; at the lower rate this explains a part of the misses,
so the served process also misses deadlines between commands. An asap command became visible
after a median of \numBfourVisPfiftyMinMs--\numBfourVisPfiftyMaxMs~ms, with p99
\numBfourVisPninetynineMinMs--\numBfourVisPninetynineMaxMs~ms (Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(c)) and rare
outliers of up to \numBfourVisMaxMs~ms. The paced schedule accounts for at most one page of
this, because an asap command takes effect at the
earliest uncomputed sample and the page holding it is released at most one page later. The
floor of about \numBfourVisMinMs~ms matches the engine's work on the page with the configuration
change, and the spread is consistent with the hand-over between the control-socket thread and
the paced loop, which share one Python interpreter. Most of the latency thus comes from the
implementation (the configuration change and the serving thread), not from the paced schedule.
Timed commands were sent with a lead of
\numBfourLeadMs~ms over the client's
estimate of virtual time, less than the command latency: \numBfourLateLow{} of
\numBfourCmdsLow{} timed commands at \numBfourRateLowHz~commands/s (from
\numBfourLateRepMinPct{} to \numBfourLateRepMaxPct~\% per repetition, depending on the error
of the client's clock estimate) and \numBfourLateMid{} of \numBfourCmdsMid{} at
\numBfourRateMidHz~commands/s arrived after their switch sample had been computed and were
applied on arrival, as designed. A timed command arrives in time when its lead exceeds the
command latency plus the error of the client's estimate of virtual time, and is late
otherwise. At
\numBfourRateHighKHz~kHz and above the engine is the bottleneck: the request-reply client
achieved \numBfourAchievedMin--\numBfourAchievedMax{} commands/s, page latencies grew to as
much as \numBfourPageLatMaxS~s, and a repetition took up to \numBfourLagPct~\% longer than its
virtual duration because the engine caught up after the client had stopped. Timed commands then
lay in the virtual future of the lagging engine, and some became visible as late as
\numBfourTimedVisMaxS~s after their receipt.

\subsection{Cost model}
\claim{C-WC-COSTMODEL}B1 fits a page cost model, a constant plus linear terms
for snapshots, fractional-delay filtering, receivers and paths, to \numBonePoints{} engine
points measured on an otherwise idle server. The fit is indicative: its relative RMS error
is \numBoneRelRmsPct~\% and its largest error \numBoneRelMaxPct~\%. The constant term,
\numBoneFixedUs~$\mu$s per page, is \numBoneFixedSharePct~\% of the single-link page cost of
\numBoneBasePageUs~$\mu$s, so short pages pay mostly fixed cost. The largest errors are
underestimates of mobility points. There, a snapshot costs about \numBoneSnapMs~ms plus
\numBoneSnapLinkMs~ms per link, with little dependence on the number of surface rows and
quadrature points $RQ$ at the sizes of these points; a diagnostic fit with these terms reaches
\numBoneExtRelRmsPct~\%, but with \numBoneExtCoeffs{} coefficients for \numBonePoints{} points it
is not used for prediction, and at the larger element counts of B2 the snapshot cost does grow
with $N$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}(a)).
The kernel itself scales as expected: the snapshot time grows as
$(RQ)^{\numBoneSnapExpNumpy}$ on the NumPy reference backend for $RQ$ from \numBoneRqMin{} to
\numBoneRqMax, and as $(RQ)^{\numBoneSnapExpNumba}$ with numba up to \numBoneRqMaxNumba, where
fixed costs dominate the small sizes.

\subsection{Cross-tool context}
\claim{C-WC-CROSSTOOL}For context, B6 times one evaluation of the same near-field surface sum
on the laptop for $N=\numBsixNmin$ to \numBsixNmax{} elements with \sremu{} and with MATLAB
\txtBsixMatlab{}; where both compute the same model the results agree to
\numBsixAgreeDb~dB. \sremu{} takes \numBsixFullMinUs--\numBsixFullMaxUs~$\mu$s for new
positions and \numBsixCombMinUs--\numBsixCombMaxUs~$\mu$s for a configuration change with the
geometry cached; a vectorized MATLAB script takes \numBsixVecMinUs--\numBsixVecMaxUs~$\mu$s, a
per-element MATLAB loop \numBsixLoopMinUs--\numBsixLoopMaxUs~$\mu$s, and the toolbox's
far-field \texttt{phased.RISReflector}, a different model,
\numBsixRisrMinUs--\numBsixRisrMaxUs~$\mu$s. These numbers are not a ranking: the calls do
different work (the \sremu{} times include Python dispatch and the line-of-sight path; the
MATLAB times exclude its start-up), they come from one machine whose other processes were busy
for \numBsixBusyPct~\% of the time during the \sremu{} timings, and a streaming emulator's cost
is per page, not per call.

\claim{C-WC-LIMITS}\emph{Limitations.} The numbers hold for the stated hosts, commit and
benchmark scenes; another host needs its own runs. B3 and B4 used one instance each with
\numBthreeReps{} repetitions per point. Paced timing was measured headless (B3) or with the
control socket alone (B4); the native data plane (F5) awaits its rerun. Packed lockstep values
are conservative by up to \numBtwoIsoMaxPct~\%, the cost model is indicative, and the laptop of
B6 was not idle. Paced mode is soft real time and its results are measured, not guaranteed.
