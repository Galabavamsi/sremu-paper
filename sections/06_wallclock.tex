\section{Wall-Clock Behaviour and Scalability}\label{sec:wallclock}

\claim{C-WC-DEFS}Lockstep runs advance virtual time as fast as the inputs and the host allow
and never drop a sample; paced runs release page $j$ at the wall time $R_j$ of
Section~\ref{sec:design-time}, so a received sample is delivered between
$D_{\mathrm{lag}}$ (\numPacedLagMs~ms by default) plus the page's computation and one page more
after its own wall time. The real-time factor (RTF) is the virtual time advanced (the minimum
over the received streams) per unit of wall time. A paced page misses its deadline at slack
$s$ when it is delivered later than $R_j+s$; a configuration is \emph{Sustainable} when, over at
least \numSustainMinDurationS~s after a warm-up of \numSustainWarmupS~s, fewer than a fraction
\numSustainMissRate{} of its pages miss the deadline at \numSustainSlackMs~ms slack, no input
sample arrives late and no output page overflows. A \emph{headless} run generates and consumes
its streams inside the emulator process, without client processes, transport or control
socket, and bounds what the engine alone achieves; a \emph{served} run exchanges samples or
commands with other processes over sockets. A link is a transmitter--receiver pair
($L=n_{\mathrm{tx}}n_{\mathrm{rx}}$). The visible latency of a command runs from its
receipt to the delivery of the page that holds its switch; a timed command is late when that
sample was already computed at its receipt, and is then applied on arrival.
\claim{C-WC-HOSTS}Lockstep benchmarks ran on AWS \txtHostServer{} instances, paced ones on
\txtHostPaced{} instances with the engine pinned to \numPacedEngineCpus{} logical CPUs, on
scenes that load the engine and carry no physical result (Table~\ref{tab:platforms},
Appendix~\ref{app:bench}).

\subsection{Lockstep scaling and data plane}\label{sec:wc-data}
\claim{C-WC-LOCKSTEP}One link at \numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s through one surface of \numBtwoBaseN{}
elements, with a static geometry and thermal noise (feature set F1), runs at RTF
\numBtwoBaseRtf; from $N=\numBtwoNmin$ to \numBtwoNmax{} this RTF stays within
\numBtwoNspreadPct~\%, because a static snapshot is computed once, while with mobility (F3) it
falls from \numBtwoFthreeRtfSmall{} to \numBtwoFthreeRtfLarge. Surfaces likewise cost little
when static and much with mobility (Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}(d)): from none to four, the one-link RTF
falls from \numBtwoRzeroStaticRtf{} to \numBtwoRfourStaticRtf{} static and from
\numBtwoRzeroMobileRtf{} to \numBtwoRfourMobileRtf{} with mobility. With one static surface, \numBtwoMaxLinks{} links keep RTF${}\geq1$ at
\numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s for every $N$. Threads help little (Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}(e)): at
\numBtwoMaxLinks{} links the RTF is \numBtwoThrOneRtf{} with one numba thread,
\numBtwoThrBestRtf{} with \numBtwoThrBest{} and \numBtwoThrMaxRtf{} with \numBtwoThrMax.
\claim{C-WC-ISOLATION}Up to \numBtwoSlots{} points ran at a time on one host; rerun alone,
reference points were up to \numBtwoIsoMaxPct~\% faster, so these values are conservative.

\claim{C-WC-DATAPLANE}Served over the native ZMQ data plane to a transmitting and a receiving
client process (F5, rerun B2-F45), the base point delivered samples end to end at RTF
\numBtwoFfiveRtf, against \numBtwoFfiveFfourRtf{} in process with recording (F4) in the same
run; over \numBtwoFfivePoints{} configurations F5 reached
\numBtwoFfiveRatioMin--\numBtwoFfiveRatioMax{} times the F4 RTF and never kept up with the wall
clock (Fig.~\ref{fig:F7}(f)), and all \numBtwoFfiveStressRuns{} stress runs of the base point
on \numBtwoFfiveStressWorkers{} concurrent workers completed. Appendix~\ref{app:bench} adds
the latency of the UHD-shaped API (E-F).

\subsection{Paced operation}
\claim{C-WC-PACED}Headless (B3), at \numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s with one surface of \numBtwoBaseN{}
elements and noise, the engine alone sustains \numBthreeOneLinks{} links with pages of
\numBthreeOnePageUs~$\mu$s (worst p99 page latency \numBthreeOnePninetynineUs~$\mu$s),
\numBthreeHalfLinks{} with \numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s and \numBthreeQuarterLinks{} with
\numBthreeQuarterPageUs~$\mu$s; \numBthreeLowLinks{} links at \numBthreeLowMsps~MS/s,
\numBthreeMidLinks{} at \numBthreeMidMsps~MS/s and one at \numBthreeTopMsps~MS/s
(Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(b)). \claim{C-WC-SHORTPAGES}These limits are indicative: link counts were
doubled until a repetition failed, \numBthreeSingleRepEnds{} of the \numBthreeSeries{} series
ended on a single repetition, and short pages fail on computation or on rare stalls
(Appendix~\ref{app:bench}). Mobility, tried at \numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s pages alone, missed
\numBthreeFthreeMissPct~\% of the deadlines with one link.

\subsection{Control loop}\label{sec:wc-loop}
\claim{C-WC-LOCKCMD}In lockstep, controllers issued \numBfourLockCommands{} commands at
\numBfourRateMinHz~Hz to \numBfourRateMaxKHz~kHz, in process and over the control socket, and
every lateness value is zero, as the barrier semantics require. From \numBfourRateMidHz~Hz on,
each in-process command costs \numBfourCmdCostMinMs--\numBfourCmdCostMaxMs~ms of engine time, so
at \numBfourRateMaxKHz~kHz
the RTF drops from \numBfourRtfNone{} to \numBfourRtfInproc{} in process and to
\numBfourRtfRemote{} over the socket.

\claim{C-WC-PACEDCMD}Served in paced mode to an external process sending configuration
commands over the control socket (B4: \numBfourLinks{} links, \numBfourPageUs~$\mu$s pages;
Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(c, d)), the engine was not backlogged at \numBfourRateLowHz{} and
\numBfourRateMidHz~commands/s. Asap commands became visible after a median of
\numBfourVisPfiftyMinMs--\numBfourVisPfiftyMaxMs~ms and a p99 of
\numBfourVisPninetynineMinMs--\numBfourVisPninetynineMaxMs~ms; pooled over repetitions, timed
commands took effect at their exact sample in at least \numBfourExactOkMinPct~\% of the cases
with leads of
\numBfourLeadOkMs~ms or more, and \numBfourLateTwoMinPct--\numBfourLateTwoMaxPct~\% were late
with \numBfourLeadMinMs~ms. No served repetition met the Sustainable rule:
\numBfourMissLowMinPct--\numBfourMissLowMaxPct~\% of the pages at \numBfourRateLowHz{} and
\numBfourMissMidMinPct--\numBfourMissMidMaxPct~\% at \numBfourRateMidHz~commands/s missed the
\numSustainSlackMs~ms deadline (at most \numBfourMissFiveMidMax{} at \numBacklogSlackMs~ms slack),
where the same links and pages missed none headless. At \numBfourRateHighHz~commands/s the
engine was the bottleneck (\numBfourAchievedMin--\numBfourAchievedMax{} commands/s achieved).
We thus regard served controller-in-the-loop operation, shown at
\numBfourRateLowHz--\numBfourRateMidHz~commands/s with leads of \numBfourLeadOkMs~ms or more, as
preliminary.

\claim{C-WC-LIMITS}\emph{Limitations.} The numbers hold for the stated hosts, commits and
scenes (one instance per benchmark, up to \numBthreeReps{} repetitions per point). Paced runs,
all on Linux, sustained at most \numBthreeOneLinks{} links at
\numBthreeLowMsps--\numPacedMSps~MS/s and one at \numBthreeTopMsps~MS/s headless; none
exchanged samples with external applications. Paced mode is soft real time; its results are measured,
not guaranteed.
