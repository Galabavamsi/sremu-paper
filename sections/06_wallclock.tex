\section{Wall-Clock Behaviour and Scalability}\label{sec:wallclock}

\claim{C-WC-DEFS}Lockstep runs advance virtual time as fast as the inputs and the host allow
and never drop a sample; paced runs release page $j$ at the wall time $R_j$ of
Section~\ref{sec:design-time}. The real-time factor (RTF) is the virtual time advanced (the
minimum over the received streams of samples produced divided by the sample rate) per unit of
wall time, so a run with RTF${}\geq1$ keeps up with the wall clock; aggregate throughput is the
received samples, summed over streams, per second of wall time. A paced page misses
its deadline at slack $s$ when the time from $R_j$ to its delivery exceeds $s$, so the miss
rate at slack $s$ is the complementary distribution of the page latency. A configuration is
\emph{Sustainable} when, over at least \numSustainMinDurationS~s after a warm-up of
\numSustainWarmupS~s, fewer than a fraction \numSustainMissRate{} of its pages miss the
deadline at \numSustainSlackMs~ms slack, no input sample arrives late and no output page
overflows. The visible latency of a command is the wall time from its receipt to the delivery
of the page that holds its switch sample; its lateness is the distance in samples between the
requested and the applied switch sample.

\claim{C-WC-HOSTS}The lockstep benchmarks (B1, B2) ran on an AWS \txtHostServer{} instance and
the paced and control-loop benchmarks (B3, B4) on two AWS \txtHostPaced{} instances, with the
engine pinned to \numPacedEngineCpus{} logical CPUs. The paced runs of B3 are headless
(streams generated and consumed in the emulator process, without client or transport costs),
and the scenes, with line-of-sight and single-bounce surface paths on every
transmitter--receiver link, load the engine and carry no physical result
(Appendix~\ref{app:bench}).

\subsection{Lockstep scaling}
\claim{C-WC-LOCKSTEP}One link at \numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s through one surface of \numBtwoBaseN{}
elements, with a static geometry and thermal noise (feature set F1), runs at RTF
\numBtwoBaseRtf. In static scenes the element count barely matters, because the geometry
snapshot is computed once: from $N=\numBtwoNmin$ to \numBtwoNmax{} the single-link RTF stays
within \numBtwoNspreadPct~\%. With mobility (F3) the snapshot is recomputed and the single-link
RTF falls from \numBtwoFthreeRtfSmall{} at $N=\numBtwoNmin$ to \numBtwoFthreeRtfLarge{} at
$N=\numBtwoNmax$. A static scene keeps RTF${}\geq1$ up to \numBtwoMaxLinks{} links at
\numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s for every $N$, and a single link keeps up at \numBtwoTopMsps~MS/s (RTF
\numBtwoTopRtf); oscillator impairments, mobility and recording lower the single-link RTF
step by step, and aggregate throughput peaks at \numBtwoAggMaxMsps~MS/s, at an RTF below one.
\claim{C-WC-ISOLATION}The points ran up to \numBtwoSlots{} at a time
on one host, and reference points rerun alone were up to \numBtwoIsoMaxPct~\% faster, so these
values are conservative (Appendix~\ref{app:bench}, which also covers the native ZMQ data plane,
F5, whose rerun is pending).

\subsection{Paced operation}
\claim{C-WC-PACED}Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(b) gives the largest Sustainable configuration of each B3
series. At \numBtwoBaseMsps~MS/s with one surface of \numBtwoBaseN{} elements and noise, the
emulator sustains \numBthreeOneLinks{} links with pages of \numBthreeOnePageUs~$\mu$s, with a
worst p99 page latency of \numBthreeOnePninetynineUs~$\mu$s and a worst miss rate of
\numBthreeOneMiss{} at \numSustainSlackMs~ms slack, and a single link is Sustainable at
\numBthreeTopMsps~MS/s (p99 \numBthreeTopPninetynineUs~$\mu$s). \claim{C-WC-SHORTPAGES}At
shorter pages the limit is not set by the computation alone but by rare stalls, and with
mobility by the pages that hold a geometry recomputation, so no mobility configuration is
Sustainable at \numBthreeHalfPageUs~$\mu$s pages (Appendix~\ref{app:bench}).

\subsection{Control loop}\label{sec:wc-loop}
\claim{C-WC-LOCKCMD}In lockstep, controllers issued \numBfourLockCommands{} commands at rates
from \numBfourRateMinHz~Hz to \numBfourRateMaxKHz~kHz, in process and over the control socket,
and every lateness value is zero, as the barrier semantics require. The loop costs wall time
instead: at \numBfourRateMidHz~Hz and above, each in-process command costs
\numBfourCmdCostMinMs--\numBfourCmdCostMaxMs~ms of engine time, so at \numBfourRateMaxKHz~kHz the RTF drops from \numBfourRtfNone{} to
\numBfourRtfInproc{} in process and to \numBfourRtfRemote{} over the socket.

\claim{C-WC-PACEDCMD}In paced mode with asynchronous commands from an external process (B4,
\numBfourLinks{} links, pages of \numBfourPageMs~ms), no repetition was Sustainable
(Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(d)): at \numBfourRateLowHz~commands/s,
\numBfourMissLowMinPct--\numBfourMissLowMaxPct~\% of the pages missed the
\numSustainSlackMs~ms deadline, against at most \numBthreeTwoLinkMissMax{} for the same links and
pages without the control socket (B3, another instance of the same type). An asap command
became visible after a median of \numBfourVisPfiftyMinMs--\numBfourVisPfiftyMaxMs~ms, with p99
\numBfourVisPninetynineMinMs--\numBfourVisPninetynineMaxMs~ms (Fig.~\ref{fig:F8}(c)) and rare
outliers of up to \numBfourVisMaxMs~ms; most of this latency comes from the implementation, not
the paced schedule: the configuration change and, as the spread suggests, the hand-over
between the control-socket thread and the paced loop, which share one Python interpreter.
Timed commands sent with a lead of
\numBfourLeadMs~ms, less than this latency, were late in \numBfourLateRepMinPct{} to
\numBfourLateRepMaxPct~\% of the cases per repetition at \numBfourRateLowHz~commands/s and were
applied on arrival, as designed: a timed command is in time when its lead exceeds the command
latency plus the error of the client's estimate of virtual time.

\claim{C-WC-COSTMODEL}A page cost model fitted in B1 is indicative (relative RMS error
\numBoneRelRmsPct~\%), with a constant term of \numBoneFixedSharePct~\% of the single-link page
cost; \claim{C-WC-CROSSTOOL}B6 times a near-field surface sum with \sremu{} and MATLAB
\txtBsixMatlab{} on a laptop as context, not as a ranking; where both compute the same model
they agree to \numBsixAgreeDb~dB (Appendix~\ref{app:bench}). \claim{C-WC-LIMITS}\emph{Limitations.} The numbers hold for
the stated hosts, commit and scenes, with one instance and \numBthreeReps{} repetitions per
point for B3 and B4; paced timing was measured headless or with the control socket alone, and
packed lockstep values are conservative by up to \numBtwoIsoMaxPct~\%. Paced mode is soft real
time; its results are measured, not guaranteed.
